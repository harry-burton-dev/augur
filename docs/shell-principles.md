# Shell principles

The rules the shell, the kit and the registry follow for getting around
AUGUR: the menus, the top bar, and moving between screens and floors. Each is
a rule, not a fix for one screen. The shell works out what to show from app
manifests and floors, and nothing in it names a particular app, so a rule
holds for apps and floors that don't exist yet.

Each rule says what holds it: a test, a registry check on manifests, or a
token. If you need to break one, change the rule here first.

| Rule | What it means | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nothing is obtuse** | AUGUR never hides something only because you can't use it right now. Anything disabled or unavailable stays where it would be, looks unavailable, and says why in plain words: who can use it, what it's waiting for, or what would turn it on. Hiding is only for things you are not allowed to know exist. This applies across the platform, not only the shell. | Kit controls carry a `reason` with their disabled state, shown on hover and focus; `pnpm check:reasons` in CI fails a platform screen that disables one without it, and the kit warns on localhost |
| **Fixed depth** | Getting around always goes app, area, group, page. Title shows apps and their areas. Orbit shows an app's groups and pages. The app shows the page. No level shows another level's things, so Title never lists an app's sub-menus or actions. | `titleMenu()` and `orbitMenu()` in `platform/frontends/shell/src/host/menus.ts`, and their tests |
| **Nothing repeats its parent** | No child has the same label or icon as the thing it sits under, at any depth. | `menuWarnings()` in the registry, run on every manifest it takes and by `pnpm check:manifest`; a registry test over the platform's own apps; a shell test over Title and Orbit |
| **One child is the parent** | A level with one child isn't offered as a choice. An app with one area, or a group with one page, opens that thing itself. | `areasOf()` and `orbitMenu()` in the shell, the centre menu's itemless category, and their tests |
| **Home and Title go there from anywhere** | Two round buttons top left, beside the floor, are the same on every screen: Home goes to Home, and All apps goes to Title, from Title, Orbit or inside any app. The one you're on is marked, and Home stays and says why when a floor hasn't got it. Esc still goes up one level, worked out from the screen (an app to its Orbit, Orbit to Title), and an app that keeps its own menu keeps Esc. None of this is wired up per app. | `navsFor()`, `navTarget()` and `backFrom()` in `platform/frontends/shell/src/host/menus.ts`, and their tests |
| **Every app stands on the same ground** | Title and Orbit sit on water. Every app sits on the one still slate ground, the one Artifacts is drawn on, whatever the app. Between screens the ground doesn't change, so moving floors inside an app never shows the water. | `waterFor()` in `platform/frontends/shell/src/App.tsx`, and the contract test "an app sits on the still ground" |
| **The bar is chrome, not a surface** | The top bar never paints a background on any screen. It stays readable over the water through one `scrim` token. The floor you're on sits top left with Home and All apps beside it, and the app's name sits under the look-up handle. | Aerie's `Shell` (`packages/shell-kit/src/shell.tsx` (augur-kit), `shell.css`) and the contract tests |
| **Every input moves both ways** | In both menus, the arrow keys, the wheel, a trackpad and a press-and-drag all move up and down through items and sideways through categories. Movement follows the hand, then settles on the nearest item. A notched mouse wheel moves one item per notch. A drag locks to one axis after 6px, and a press that moves less than that is still a click. | Aerie's `CentreMenu` (`centre-menu.tsx`) and the contract tests |
| **Reachable means readable** | Any menu icon that is shown and can be clicked keeps at least 55% opacity, so it keeps 3:1 contrast over the water. Things further away fade out completely rather than sitting faint. | Aerie's `CentreMenu` (`SHOWN` in `centre-menu.tsx`) |
| **Menus are lists, not applications** | A menu's categories are a tablist and the focused category's items a listbox that holds focus and points at the focused item, so screen readers announce where you are without a special mode. | Aerie's `CentreMenu` and the contract tests |
| **The choice carries you** | Every move between levels or floors is one motion: what you chose goes from where it was to where it lands, in the direction of the move. Menus and floors share it. | `liftGhost()` in `packages/shell-kit/src/motion.ts` (augur-kit) and the centre menu's `leave()`, with their tests. On a floor move the screen leaves the way you're going and the next comes in from the other side (`leaveFloor()` on Aerie's `ShellModel`) |
| **A floor move that changes nothing isn't offered** | In an app that shows the same on every floor, the floor gauge rests. It stays in view, dimmed, and pointing at it says why, rather than moving you to a floor that looks the same. The app says so once in its manifest (`sameOnEveryFloor`), and no app is special-cased in the shell. | `ShellModel.floorsRest` in Aerie; Admin's entry in `platform/services/registry/src/manifest.ts` |
| **Each level of team is its own level** | Teams right below the org are one level and those inside them another: Department, then Team, unless an admin renames them in Admin → Departments → Level names. Each level has its own name everywhere the floor is named (the title card, the gauge, Account, the top left for screen readers), its own icon, and its own water colours (`floor-department-*`, then `floor-team-*`). Depth decides the level, so no team is marked by hand, and the kind stays `team` for apps. | `levelName()` in `platform/services/identity/src/workspaces.ts`; `floorTone()` and `floorKindOf()` in Aerie (`packages/shell-kit/src/floors.ts` in the `augur-kit` repository), and their tests |
| **Arrivals are generated** | The area title card shown on arriving at a floor or opening an app is built from that floor's or app's own data: its kind or level, name, and who sees it. No card is written by hand. An app's card fades out where it is. A card plays alone: what's behind it, the app or the floor's screen, stays unseen until the card has faded, then fades in. | Aerie's `ShellModel` builds the card from the floor's kind, name and who sees it, or from the app and the floor it opened on, and its tests. A kicker that would only repeat the name is left off. It ignores the pointer, and any key, press or scroll ends it. The Area titles setting turns cards off |
| **One motion switch** | The system's reduce-motion setting and AUGUR's own are one check. Every motion has a crossfade fallback, and loops stop. | Kit `motionMode()` and its tests; the reduce block in the kit's `base.css` sets every motion token to near zero and stops loops. A key or press during a move jumps it to its end (`finishMotion()`) |
| **The shell never opens itself** | A new notification changes the status words and pulses the point once; nothing pops up over what you're doing. Someone who wants more can turn on Toasts for new notifications in Settings, and each new one is then said once at the bottom of the screen with Open. It holds while hovered or focused and goes by itself after `motion-toast`. A muted app stays silent either way. | `ShellModel.notify()` in Aerie; `toastFor()` in `platform/frontends/shell/src/host/toasts.ts` and its test; Aerie's `Toasts`. The `toasts` preference is off unless true |
| **Urgent is letterboxed** (coming) | Harry's principle, word for word: "**Aspect Ratio Framing:** Urgent and important information use a cinematic, hyper-focused letterbox (2.39:1 widescreen viewport) to eliminate peripheral clutter". It is a kit rule, not one screen's: Aerie's full-screen notices use it, and apps get the same frame from Quill. Nothing outside the frame is hidden for good; it steps back while the urgent thing is shown. | PDR-009 in the handbook. `Letterbox` in Common (so in Aerie and Quill), its ratio from the `letterbox-ratio` token and its bars from `scrim`. Coming: the shell's own notices move to it with Aerie |
| **Depth, not scrims** | Harry's principle (PDR-010): don't fight for flat screen space, use shallow depth of field and layers. When focus moves to an active surface (a dialog, the palette, the radial menu), the layout stays in place and the plane behind loses fine detail and colour and shrinks a touch, as a lens refocuses. No black scrim, so nobody loses their bearings. Ambient things can sit softly out of focus while something that needs action pulls focus to itself, and hovering or focusing one brings it back at once. | `FocusStage` (in every `AppFrame`), `useRack`, `FocusField` and `FocusItem` in Quill, with the `depth-*` and `ambient-*` tokens; Quill's CSS racks Common's dialogs too. Reduced transparency falls back to the `veil`, reduced motion drops the shrink. Tests in `app-kit/src/depth.test.tsx` |
| **The path you took is lit** | In a tree, the place you open opens in place and its neighbours dim, so only the path down to where you are is lit, joined by a line. Nothing is hidden: the neighbours stay where they were, readable at 4.5:1, and come up on hover and focus. | `PathTree` in Quill and the `path-dim` token, with its tests |
| **Actions live on the object** | Every action an object has is in its radial menu (right-click, the context-menu key, Shift+F10 or a long press), round the object, which stays sharp while the rest racks out. Unavailable actions stay with their reasons; danger goes last; past eight it's a list. | `RadialMenu` and `useRingTarget` in Quill, with their tests |
| **Tokens only** | Every duration, distance and colour comes from the token files in `packages/kit-common/tokens/` in the `augur-kit` repository; in React, through the Tailwind theme built from them, which has nothing else in it. Icons come from Lucide through Common's `Icon`. | Token build; the theme test in `kit-common`; review |

Rules marked "coming" are agreed and being built. Until they land, the
behaviour they describe isn't there yet.

Where the shell hides things today, it is to be measured against **Nothing is
obtuse**: an app offered only on some kinds of floor, a menu entry that needs
a role, and an empty menu should show as unavailable with the reason, unless
knowing it exists is itself restricted.
