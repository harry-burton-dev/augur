# Below the bar, with a hedge

A decision often has to be taken before it can be proven (notice is due whatever we know). It may then go ahead below its kind's standard of proof only if every assumption below the bar has a written hedge (what we do if it is false, who does it) and a sign that would trigger it, all agreed at the table. The decision says it was decided below its bar, names its weakest assumption, and carries the hedge, so nobody has to invent the response on the day the sign shows. From Dewar's assumption-based planning (hedging actions and signposts) and Munger's inversion.
