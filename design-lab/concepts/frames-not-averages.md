# Frames, not averages

When two people read the same data differently, both frames are kept side by side, each with its author, the cues it picks, what it predicts, and the result that would change its author's mind, written before any test. They are never averaged. A test agreed in advance settles them, each rule applies itself, and the frame is rewritten with the old reading kept. The decider reads agreed facts above and issues in dispute below, each with what would decide it. From Klein, Moon and Hoffman's data/frame theory, Weick, double crux, pre-registration, statements of agreed facts and bank reconciliation.
