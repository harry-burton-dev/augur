# Alerts and escalation: critique

Surface: how something moves from ambient to "needs you" to urgent, and the urgent notice itself (PDR-009 letterbox).
Six prototypes, all checked at 1600×900 and 1280×800 with and without reduced motion. No console errors. Every page: auto-demo after `--motion-run`, `R` replays, `M` toggles reduced motion for review, and sample data sits in one `DATA` object.

Shared across all six (worth turning into kit parts): a `Letterbox` (two `--scrim`-gradient bars with blur, frame sized from `--letterbox-ratio`, bars that carry metadata), a `FarPlane` wrapper (`blur + saturate + brightness + scale(.995)`, `translateZ(0)`, `will-change`), a four-part `Explain` block (what happened / why it matters / what you can do / who else can act) and a `DisabledAction` (dashed button plus a lock line saying why and who can change it).

---

## V1: Letterbox arrival (`v1-letterbox-arrival.html`)
*Idea.* This is the plain version of PDR-009. Bars slide in from top and bottom, the app racks back in place (blur, desaturate, 0.995 shrink), and the notice resolves from blur into the 2.39 frame in stages: clock, then headline, explanation and actions. The bars carry information rather than being dead space: the top bar holds the kind, sequence, floor, source and a live clock; the bottom bar holds who has seen it and the keys. Esc steps back to a pill at the top right that stays until resolved. Acting sends a sweep along the frame edge, the tone turns from amber to green, the explanation is replaced by "what changed / who was told / undo", and then the frame opens again with a toast.
*Sources.* Territory Studio (telemetry at the edges, short caps), Make It So (confirmation through motion), PDR-009.
*What works.* It is calm and readable, and the hierarchy holds at both sizes. Using the bars for metadata answers the cost of the letterbox: the space it takes is not wasted. Racking back without a black scrim keeps your place, because you can still see the shapes of the Artifacts home behind the frame. The success state is the best part, because it shows the consequences instead of "Done!".
*What fails or risks.* At 1280×800 the frame is 535 px tall, and four explanation cells plus two rows of actions only just fit. Longer copy will overflow, so the kit part needs a rule for that (a "more" disclosure, not scrolling inside a letterbox). The big clock duplicates the headline's time. The arrival takes about 1.1 s in total, which feels cinematic the first time and slow the tenth. The pill must not become a second notification system.
*Principles.* Letterbox: yes, by the book. Focus stacking: far plane plus resolve-from-blur on arrival. Explain, never hide: all four parts, a disabled action with its policy and owner, and the pill instead of dismissal.
*Needs.* `--x-rack-blur`, `--x-rack-saturate`, `--x-rack-dim`, `--x-rack-scale` tokens. Data on "seen by" and "told at" for notices, which the shell doesn't have yet.
*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 5

## V2: Dark cockpit annunciator (`v2-annunciator.html`)
*Idea.* A glareshield across the top with two master buttons and a 16-cell annunciator. In the normal state the panel is dark ("dark cockpit"), and only abnormal cells light up. A caution lights amber and flashes until acknowledged (Space presses the master caution). A warning lights red, and the shield becomes the first row of the letterbox's top bar, with the lit cell repeated as a large lamp beside the notice. An EICAS-style message list on the right gives the short text, ▸ for unacknowledged items, and a line naming systems you are not allowed to see. Clicking an amber cell drops a "needs you" card and softens the app without a letterbox.
*Sources.* Avionics master caution and warning, the EICAS message list, Rams (discrete push states), Territory Studio's numbered segments (the 7-step rollout strip).
*What works.* Acknowledging is separate from resolving, which is the most important idea on this surface and missing from most UIs: you can say "I've seen it" without being forced to act. The dark default makes any light meaningful. The locked "Models" cell (hatched, with a lock and the reason) is a strong example of explain-never-hide for things you may not open. The step strip states progress at a glance.
*What fails or risks.* Flashing is the most alarm-fatigue-prone pattern in this set. I held it to 900 ms per cycle (about 1.1 Hz, well under the 3 Hz photosensitivity limit) and to master buttons and cells only, and it stops on acknowledgement. Reduced motion swaps the flash for a double outline. Sixteen fixed cells is an avionics habit: AUGUR's systems vary per deployment, so the grid must come from the manifest, and with 30 systems it becomes a wall. A permanent band at the top costs 64 px on every screen, which could only be justified for admins.
*Principles.* Letterbox: only for warnings. Cautions get a card plus a soft far plane, so the ladder is built into the levels. Explain, never hide: locked cells, "not shown to you" lines, and a "never skip a migration" reason that says nobody can turn the rule off.
*Needs.* An acknowledgement model in the events service (ack ≠ resolve, who acked and when). A manifest-driven list of annunciator systems. A severity token pair (`--x-caution`, `--x-warning`) only if `--warning` and `--danger` are not enough. A role gate: admins only.
*Score.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 3

## V3: Escalation ladder on a lens focus scale (`v3-escalation-ladder.html`)
*Idea.* One item followed through its life, a library filling up: ambient (a soft, blurred row among other read-only rows), notice (a toast, row sharpened), needs you (the same surface travels to the centre as a focused card and the app racks back), urgent (letterbox). A vertical focus-distance scale on the right (∞ · 10 · 3 · 1, like the barrel of a cine lens) shows the current level, and a level selector at the top lets you jump. Space escalates, Shift+Space de-escalates. A segmented meter (green to amber to red cells) is the shared visual at every level, so you recognise the item as it grows.
*Sources.* Cine lens focus scale, Eurorack LED meters, Harry's focus-pulling rule.
*What works.* It best expresses "one item, many depths": the same meter and wording appear at every level, so escalation never looks like a new, unrelated alert. The lens scale also teaches people the system: after one look you know there are four levels and where you are. The urgent bar carries the history ("ambient 05:50 · notice 06:10 · needs you 06:31 · urgent 07:12", "escalated after 41 min without action"), which makes escalation explainable.
*What fails or risks.* Blurring ambient rows hurts if you actually want to read them; it only works with focus-on-hover. The toast travelling to the centre is a large move, so it needs the reduced-motion crossfade (provided), and some people find even a 600 ms travel uncomfortable. The lens scale is a demo device: in the product it should probably be a small depth indicator on the item, not a permanent rail.
*Principles.* All three: ambient blur, focus pull, letterbox, and a level that is always visible.
*Needs.* Escalation rules per item kind (thresholds, time without action) in the events service, an "escalation history" on each notice, and a `--x-ambient-blur` token (I used a fifth of the rack blur).
*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V4: Closing aperture (`v4-countdown.html`)
*Idea.* A deadline whose letterbox is not binary: the aperture closes continuously as the latest safe start approaches. At 20 minutes out there is only an Open MCT-style timeline strip (window, approvals, rollout lanes, a NOW line, and a "latest safe start" line). From about 14 minutes out the bars begin to grow, the far plane blurs and dims, and the decision fades in. In the last minutes the frame is fully closed with a large, thin countdown. The demo fast-forwards ×480 and drops to real time for the last 3 minutes. Esc pushes it back until 2 minutes are left, and approving releases the frame and turns the rollout lane green.
*Sources.* Make It So (countdowns for urgency), Open MCT (timeline lanes, NOW marker), avionics "time to go".
*What works.* It counts to the honest deadline (latest *safe start*, 00:15) instead of the obvious one (window close, 01:00), and says why. The calm is real: there is no red until the end and no pulsing, only the space closing in. "If nobody approves" states that nothing breaks tonight, which lowers panic and alarm fatigue.
*What fails or risks.* A continuously closing frame is ambient anxiety: the screen tightens while you work on something else, which some people will hate. It needs a per-person ceiling, "never past needs-you unless the deadline is under X". Updating `filter` on every frame across a large DOM is expensive, so in production, step `--ap` in 5–10 discrete detents (also more Rams). The blend from 14 minutes to 3 has a stretch where the decision text sits over a still-readable app, and legibility drops there.
*Principles.* Letterbox as a function of urgency, not a state. That is an extension of PDR-009 and should be a new Proposed record if adopted.
*Needs.* `--x-aperture-steps` (detents), deadline and latest-safe-start data on approvals (the updater knows the rollout duration; the shell doesn't), and a mute ceiling per person.
*Score.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V5: Limit violation (`v5-limit-violation.html`)
*Idea.* A Health board of small multiples (8 measures, limit lines, one marked *stale* with hatching and a struck-through last value). The events backlog crosses its yellow limit (the tile border and value turn amber; no interruption), then its red limit. The plot itself then expands from the tile into the letterbox, because 2.39:1 is the natural shape for a time series. Inside, the full plot shows yellow and red bands, the line coloured by band (clip paths), an annotation for the likely cause ("Upgrade 4.3 · step 3 began"), NOW, a dashed projection to the hard limit with "hard limit in ~N min", and a live readout. The four-part explanation sits on a veiled panel to the left. Pausing consumers bends the line down live.
*Sources.* NASA Open MCT (limit bands, staleness, NOW), Tufte (small multiples, data-ink, micro/macro), Bloomberg (dense, keyboard-first).
*What works.* The violation is shown in context, with cause and trend, which a textual alert can't do. The tile-to-frame expansion is the clearest "this thing came forward" in the set, because it really is the same object. Yellow is quiet and red interrupts, which is a correct split for alarm fatigue. Stale data is visible and explained rather than shown as a flat line.
*What fails or risks.* The panel covers the left third of the plot (the quiet past); acceptable, but on a narrow screen the ramp could slide under it. The projection is naive (a linear rate over 10 samples) and can mislead, so it needs a confidence or "if this rate continues" wording (I used the latter). Live-updating SVG inside a blurred far plane is fine for one plot, but don't do it for 50. Only admins and operators should ever see this level.
*Principles.* Letterbox, focus stacking (tile → frame) and explain-never-hide (stale marking, the disabled hard limit with who can change it).
*Needs.* A telemetry and limits API (limits per measure per deployment), event annotations from the updater, and a kit `Plot` with limit bands. A dataviz decision too: there is no chart library ADR yet.
*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V6: Level-3 approval as three focal planes (`v6-approval.html`)
*Idea.* The approval overlay becomes a triptych inside the letterbox: on the left, **how it got here** (chain of custody: drafted, reviewed, legal check, requested, you; plus the v3→v4 word diff); in the centre, **the decision** (a floor ladder You · Team · Department · Org showing the move, the question in one sentence, *hold to publish*); on the right, **what happens if you approve** (214 people as 214 dots, replaces v3, notifications, how to undo). ← and → rack focus between planes: the chosen one sharpens and the others blur and shrink. Holding Enter fills a line along the button for `--motion-run`; releasing early cancels. On confirm, the 214 dots light in sequence and the next approval in the queue arrives. The second item gives someone admin, and its consequences list what they *can* and *cannot* do.
*Sources.* Make It So (confirmation through motion), rack focus, chain-of-custody documents, Tufte (one dot per person).
*What works.* Provenance and consequence give the approver what they actually need, instead of "Approve?". The dots make reach physical, so publishing to 214 people feels different from 38. The floor ladder states floor travel without words. Hold-to-confirm suits level-3 decisions: it is deliberate without an extra dialog, and you can let go.
*What fails or risks.* Rack focus inside the frame is a gimmick risk: blurring provenance while you decide hides the very information you need, so the default keeps all three planes sharp and only dims the sides. Hold-to-confirm is a known accessibility problem (motor impairments, switch users), so it needs an alternative such as a typed confirmation or a setting to turn holds off. 214 dots is fine; 4,000 is not, and that case needs a scaled glyph. At 1280 the provenance details are hidden by a height rule.
*Principles.* Letterbox for important (not urgent) items, focus stacking at two depths (app, then planes within the frame), and explain-never-hide (the disabled "Publish to the whole Org", who can do it, and the policy that ends grants).
*Needs.* Provenance and reach data on approvals (registry and events), a `HoldButton` kit part with an accessible alternative, and `--x-rack-near` (a lighter in-frame blur).
*Score.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## Cross-cutting findings
- **Alarm fatigue.** Only V2 flashes, and it stops on acknowledgement. Everything else escalates through *space* (blur, framing) and not through motion or colour loops. I'd make that a rule: an urgent thing gets the frame, never a loop. The letterbox should be rare; if more than one or two a week reach a person, the thresholds are wrong.
- **Motion sickness.** The far-plane scale (0.995) is too small to feel. The large moves are V3's toast-to-centre travel and V5's tile-to-frame expansion. All of them crossfade under reduced motion; V4's continuous aperture also stays still under reduced motion apart from the numbers.
- **Acknowledge ≠ resolve ≠ dismiss.** V2 shows that three verbs are needed. Esc in every variant means "step back", never "dismiss": the item stays reachable (pill, lit lamp, red tile, Actions row).
- **Two letterboxes at once.** None of these solves it. Proposal: a queue in the top bar ("1 of 2", as V6 does), never stacked frames.
- **Gaps in the kit and tokens.** Rack-focus tokens (`--x-rack-blur`, `--x-rack-saturate`, `--x-rack-dim`, `--x-rack-scale`). `--font-mono`, which these pages rely on heavily for bars, numerals and tags. A type size below `--type-small` would help the dense annunciator and tile labels. There is no thin or hairline width token (I used 1px borders) and no severity tones distinct from `--warning`/`--danger`. Letterbox bar heights are derived (`max(--letterbox, (100vh − 100vw/2.39)/2)`), which is worth naming as a token.

## Ranking
1. **V1 Letterbox arrival:** the baseline to ship. It is clear, calm, cheap, and makes the bars earn their space.
2. **V5 Limit violation:** the most persuasive use of 2.39:1 (a plot is born widescreen) and the strongest "same object came forward".
3. **V6 Level-3 approval:** the right content model for approvals (provenance, decision, consequence); keep the planes sharp by default.
4. **V3 Escalation ladder:** the best explanation of the whole system; the lens scale is a teaching device, not chrome.
5. **V4 Closing aperture:** a fresh idea and honest deadlines, but risky as ambient pressure; ship it as detents with a ceiling.
6. **V2 Annunciator:** the most distinctive, and ack ≠ resolve is a must-take, but flashing and a permanent glareshield fit admins only.

## What I'd build first
I'd build V1's `Letterbox` as the single kit part, with the bars as slots (top: kind, floor, source, clock; bottom: seen-by, keys). I'd add the `FarPlane` wrapper and the four-part `Explain` plus `DisabledAction` blocks, since every variant reuses them. Then I'd use V6's content model for the shell's level-3 approvals (provenance on the left, consequence on the right, decision in the middle, all sharp), with a plain confirm button first and the hold as an option. Two data changes unlock most of the rest: acknowledgement state on events (from V2) and escalation history on notices (from V3). V5's plot-into-frame should come next for Admin's health board, once there is a chart library decision.
