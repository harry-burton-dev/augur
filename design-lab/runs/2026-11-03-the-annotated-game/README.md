A design lab run of 3 November 2026, wildcard W6 from batch 6, "thinking made visible": how people could **review a finished decision the way chess players review a game**. Replay it move by move, mark the critical moments with a small shared notation, keep a computed view beside the human marks with their disagreements shown, and file the annotated game so the next person facing the same kind of situation meets it. Sources: the Chess Informant symbols, annotated game collections (Nimzowitsch, Kasparov), de Groot's *Thought and Choice in Chess*, engine evaluation bars and opening explorers, ECO codes and opening theory, the after-action review, and the decision-science work on outcome bias (Baron and Hershey; Annie Duke's "resulting").

The run has 36 prototypes over six surfaces (the notation sheet, replaying a decision, marking, two voices, the collection, learning from it) and four combined screens. The game is the minute run's **D-118**: a contract decision to move van inspections in-house, made on 12 May 2026 and reversed on 3 November after 175 days. Here it is seventeen moves, twelve of ours and five of the world's. The review is held on 24 November and agreed on 8 December. A year later, on 9 November 2027, Leah Grant, a new contracts officer, drafts the same kind of renewal and meets the annotated game at the move where it turned.

The run's position, in one line: **people mark moves, the records mark positions**. A human `?!` judges a choice with what was known then; the computed view reads where the decision stood against the lines it set itself. They answer different questions, so a human mark sits beside worked-out confidence without undermining it.

Each page plays its moment about 1.2 s after load. **R** replays and **T** shows the rules and record names; other keys are on each page.
