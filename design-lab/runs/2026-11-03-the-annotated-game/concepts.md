# Concepts found by this run

Each is bigger than one prototype and could steer a future run. They are also filed in `design-lab/concepts/`.

## People mark moves, records mark positions (`moves-and-positions`)
A review has two voices that answer different questions. The records say where a decision **stood** on any date, worked out by rule from the lines it set itself (weakest line wins, never typed). People say whether each **choice** was sound, with what was known then, in a small fixed notation (`!! ! !? ?! ? ??`), signed. The two are never averaged or scored together; where they pull apart, the split is a finding, filed under one of four reasons (the records can't see it, hindsight, the line was set wrong, two people differ). A person's only mark on a position is `∞`, "the records can't see this", which lowers certainty and never moves the number. This narrows *confidence is worked out* to claims and positions rather than overturning it. Evidence: grammar V2, V4 and V1, voices V1, V3 and V5, combined V2. Sources: Chess Informant symbols, engine evaluation bars. Could steer: the argument (Z), the analyst's bench (AA), any screen that shows a model's or the records' view beside a person's.

## Judged as it stood (`judged-as-it-stood`)
A choice is judged by what was known when it was made, not by how it turned out. A replay shows each step as it stood that day: what was on the record, what was still unknown (as honest blanks), the records' view as at that date; the steps still to come sit in the far focal plane, out of focus. Hindsight is available, on its own wash, behind a key, after the mark. A `?` (mistake) needs an alternative that was on the record at the time; without one it is `?!` at most. Evidence: replay V2 and V1, voices V4, grammar V5, combined V2. Sources: Baron and Hershey, "Outcome bias in decision evaluation" (1988); Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets* ("resulting"); Fischhoff on hindsight bias. Could steer: Upgrade Centre incident reviews, approvals audits, the case file (U).

## Own moves first (`own-moves-first`)
The person who made a move marks it before anyone else does, and the reviewer's mark on it stays sealed (visible as a sealed card, saying who and when it opens) until they release theirs. Both marks then stand, signed, side by side; the mover may reply in a line but the marks don't change. The meeting agrees the review as a record, not each mark, and no page anywhere counts anyone's marks. From self-annotated games (masters annotate their own games first, and those notes are the ones read), the after-action review's rule that those who were there speak first, and blind second marking. Evidence: marking V2 and V4, grammar V6, combined V1 and V2. Could steer: approvals, performance-adjacent features that must not become them, the commission (V) sign-off.

## The way back is recorded (`the-way-back-is-recorded`)
A step that can't be undone is marked as one-way **when it is taken**, with its way back and what that costs, what it depends on, and the clock it runs against. Then the records can say on the day "a one-way step was taken before what it depends on", and a review doesn't have to find it afterwards. D-118 was lost on 28 May when notice went to Kestrel before any fitter was hired; the records' own lines read `±` for two more months. With the step recorded they would have read `=` that day. Also: options on the table at each big step, and assumptions with a date to test them. From the engine horizon, chess's "only move" and time trouble, and critical path. Evidence: voices V5, replay V3 and V4, grammar V5, combined V1 (scene 1). Could steer: Upgrade Centre (an upgrade's way back), Admin changes, consequence preview, the wake.

## The opening book (`the-opening-book`)
Agreed reviews are filed by the **situation** they were, not the subject they were about: a few facets answered when a decision is drafted (what it changes, what it depends on, its one-way step, trial or for good), with codes as optional names for common combinations, owned per deployment. Found by position, so transpositions surface (an out-of-hours phone line moved to a contractor is the same game as van inspections moved in-house). Each code's page carries its book line (the order of moves the reviewed games support), its games, motifs in opposed pairs, theory (answers to questions reviews asked), and every count with its base ("four games: not a rate, read them"). From ECO codes, opening theory and explorers, de Groot's chunks. Evidence: collection V2, V1 and V4, combined V3, voices V6. Could steer: the index (P), the catalogue's facets, Mandate's decision kinds.

## Play it first (`play-it-first`)
A precedent reaches the next person inside their task, at the step where it turned, and nowhere else: while their draft follows a reviewed game, the margin is silent ("in book"); at the matching step it speaks once. Before reading what happened, they play that moment as it stood and choose, privately; then they see what was played, the marks (including the mover's own) and the line another game played. The lesson becomes a few questions on their draft, each citing its move and each keepable with a reason; changes carry a seam naming the game, so the meeting sees where the lesson came from. From de Groot's choice-of-move studies, "guess the move" training, case method and decision-forcing cases. Evidence: learning V1, V2 and V6, combined V4 and V1. Could steer: onboarding, the apprentice's agents learning from reviewed work, Mandate drafting.

Also advanced: *confidence is worked out* (argued against in scope only: never typed for claims and positions; choices are judged by named people, in a fixed notation, kept apart), *revisit lines* (the computed view reads only a decision's own lines; "no lines, no evaluation"; when an action's date moves, its line should move or the meeting says why), *agreed in the room* (reviews agreed like minutes; assent to the record, not the mark), *decided versus said* (the critical move was an assumption said in passing; record it with a test date), *focal planes* (the future as the far plane of a replay), *time as an address* (every replay panel is "as at"), *visible repair* (a corrected mark is a new dated mark; a draft changed after a precedent keeps a seam naming the game), *mine versus ours* (own marks until released, learners' guesses never stored, no tally by person, locked reviews shown hatched), *the answer has a base* (book counts always with their base), *question and answer* (a split becomes one question to one person, no chasing), *pencil and ink* (the records' account by template, the model only ever drafts), *history as a material* (a decision's history replayed as moves, its review part of the record).

Related concepts from parallel runs: *the warrant shows* (batch 6) meets the four parts of a line of commentary (known then, chosen, instead on the record, with the idea); *the technique fits the question* (batch 6) meets the after-action review as the routine that makes marking happen.
