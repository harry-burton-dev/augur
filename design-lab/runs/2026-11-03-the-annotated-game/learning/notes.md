# Surface 6 · Learning from it

**The moment:** 9 November 2027, a year after D-118 was reversed. Leah Grant, contracts officer since September, drafts D-163: the south depot's 14 vans move from Brackley Fleet Services (£192 a van, contract ends 31 March 2028, three months' notice) to the North bay at £118. Her draft's steps: a costing with no cover; two more fitters needed by 1 March; notice to Brackley by 31 December. It is the same position as R-07, and her third step is R-07's move 6.

**Shared data:** `DATA.later`: her draft (moves with `like`, the R-07 move each one matches), the puzzle (R-07 move 6 as it stood, three choices, the one played and the book line), and the people who played R-07 with what each can speak to and whether they are still here (Priya left in March 2027).

**Keys:** N adds her next step (V1), A B C choose (V2), → next card (V4), click a person (V5), Space answers the next question (V6), R replays.

---

## V1: In book
*Idea.* While she drafts, a margin follows her steps. Steps 1 and 2 are "in book · R-07 move 1, move 4", and the margin says nothing ("Nothing to say yet"). When she adds step 3, notice by 31 December, the step edges in warning ("out of book") and the margin speaks once: R-07 played this move and it was where the game turned, the reviewer's line, and the book line from R-03 and R-11 (hire first, then notice). Three choices: replay R-07 from move 4, ask Dana who played it, or keep the move with a reason.

*Sources.* Opening books and being "out of book"; the next step is shown (switchboard run); answer, don't announce (room tone).

*What works.* The precedent arrives at the move it is about, not as reading before she starts. Silence while she is in book is the calm default. "Keep my move, with a reason" respects her: the book is advice, not a gate. The margin tells nobody what she wrote.

*What fails or risks.* Recognising a position from a draft depends on facets being set; AUGUR "recognised from her answers" needs a short set of questions when a draft is started. Matching a step to a precedent's move (`like`) is easy here and hard in general; it may need her to confirm "this is like R-07 move 6".

*Principles.* Calm: speaks once. Explain, never hide. The next step is shown. No letterbox (rightly: not urgent).

*Needs.* Facets on a draft decision; step matching against the book; the margin as a Mandate drafting part.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V2: Your move
*Idea.* Before reading how R-07 went, she plays its critical moment herself. Left, the position as it stood on 28 May 2026: what was known, and the records' view that day (`±`). Right: "You are the contracts manager. What do you do?" Three choices. After she picks, the tags show what was played (and its `?`), what R-03 played (kept), and the marks, including Dana's own `□`. The note answers her choice without grading it. Private: nothing is stored.

*Sources.* De Groot's choice-of-move experiments (players think aloud on a position); "guess the move" training in chess magazines; case teaching in business schools.

*What works.* Learning by choosing, then seeing, is how players learn from games, and it is the strongest teaching idea in the run. It makes the lesson stick because she feels the pull of the move that was played ("Most people do: the decision said 1 September, and the notice clock was running."). Dana's `□` keeps it fair to the person who played it.

*What fails or risks.* A puzzle is only as good as the position written for it; someone has to write "known that day" well, and that is the reviewer's job. Some will find it a game, and in a serious workplace that may grate; the tone must stay plain.

*Principles.* Mine versus ours: private by design. Calm. Explain, never hide.

*Needs.* A puzzle per critical moment (generated from the review's "known then", "chosen", "instead"); no storage of answers.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V3: Two boards
*Idea.* Her draft and the precedent side by side, step for step. Same moves where she plays what R-07 played (costing, dependency, notice: the last marked `?`); dashed gaps where R-07 had a good move she lacks (the trial `!`, the line `!`). A summary: three steps the same, two good moves missing, her costing counts no cover either.

*Sources.* Comparing two games move by move; transpositions.

*What works.* It finds what V1 can't: the good moves she hasn't played. "No trial" and "no line yet" are the most useful findings for her, and nothing in her draft would have prompted them.

*What fails or risks.* Comparing a draft to one precedent may overfit: R-07 is one game. Alignment by kind of step is manual here.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide. Calm.

*Needs.* Step kinds on decision steps (costing, trial, line, dependency, one-way step).

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V4: Five minutes with R-07
*Idea.* The game as a short read: the opening in one line, four cards for the critical moments (date, marks, the records' position, the reviewer's line, a one-line lesson), then keep and change. Reading progress is private.

*Sources.* Annotated game collections; the after-action review's keep and change; the reader's minute (briefing).

*What works.* Five minutes is a budget a new starter will spend. The lessons under each card are what she will remember ("Secure what it depends on before the step you can't undo."). Keep and change are the review's own words.

*What fails or risks.* It is a summary, and summaries flatten disagreement; move 13's two marks are kept, but the card is small. It reads like training material, which is fine, but lacks the pull of V2.

*Principles.* Calm. Explain, never hide.

*Needs.* Lessons per critical moment (the review's "change" items).

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 4

## V5: Ask who played it
*Idea.* The precedent's players: Dana (contracts manager, now her manager, move 6 `□`), Jo (move 4 `?!`), Ade (moves 2 and 13), Nadia Kerr (played R-03's book line), and Priya (left March 2027: can't be asked; her mark and line stay). She writes one question to Dana with move 6 attached. It waits as a question until Dana answers; nobody chases it.

*Sources.* Master and apprentice; the after-action review (ask those who were there); works under someone (apprentice run).

*What works.* "An annotated game is only half the lesson; the players are the other half." The question is about the move, not the person: "Asking about a move is never a complaint about the person who made it." Showing who has left, and that their notes stay, is explain-never-hide applied to people.

*What fails or risks.* Questions to the people who made a mistake can feel like reopening it. Dana's answer (in combined V1) is generous; not everyone's will be. Private by default means the answer helps one person unless Dana adds it to the review.

*Principles.* Mine versus ours. Question and answer. Calm.

*Needs.* A question with a `Ref` to a review move, routed through the Inbox; "add to the review as a reply".

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V6: What it asks of your draft
*Idea.* The precedent becomes three questions on her draft, each citing the move it comes from: Is what it depends on in place before the one-way step? (move 6, R-03); does the costing count cover? (move 1, R-02); is it a trial with an end date and a line? (moves 2 and 3). She changes two steps (seams on the draft say what changed and which game it came from) and keeps one with a reason ("nothing to go back to"), adding a line instead.

*Sources.* After-action "change" items; opening theory; visible repair (seams).

*What works.* Questions, not rules: each can be kept with a reason and none blocks the draft. The seams carry the lesson into the meeting, so the next review of D-163 can see it. "Questions come only from agreed reviews and the book's theory, never from a model."

*What fails or risks.* Three questions is right; thirty would be a compliance checklist, which is exactly what this run argues against. Someone will want to make them mandatory.

*Principles.* Visible repair. Explain, never hide. Calm.

*Needs.* Theory and change items linked to step kinds; seams on drafts (Mandate).

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## Ranking
1. **V1 In book**: the precedent at the move it is about, silent otherwise.
2. **V2 Your move**: play it before you read it.
3. **V6 What it asks of your draft**: questions with reasons, carried as seams.
4. **V5 Ask who played it**: the players as half the lesson.
5. **V4 Five minutes with R-07**: the reading form.
6. **V3 Two boards**: useful for the missing good moves; merge into V1.

## What I'd build first
The margin on Mandate's draft (V1), silent while in book and speaking once at the move where a precedent turned, offering "play it first" (V2) and then the three questions (V6) with seams. V5's "ask who played it" is a button in the margin. V3's missing good moves become a fourth kind of margin note ("R-07 had a trial here").
