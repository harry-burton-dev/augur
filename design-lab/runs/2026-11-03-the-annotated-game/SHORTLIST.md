# Shortlist: the annotated game

Ranked by what I'd build first. The headline: **people mark moves, the records mark positions, and the review lives on the decision.** A reviewer's `?!` judges a choice with what was known that day; the computed view reads where the decision stood against the lines it set itself, as at that day. Neither is averaged into the other, and their disagreements are named and turned into questions. Agreed reviews are filed by situation, and the next person meets one in the margin of their draft, at the move where it turned, and plays that moment before reading what happened.

**Answers to the prompt's questions:**
- *Does a human mark of judgement belong beside worked-out confidence, or does it undermine it?* **It belongs beside it, because it answers a different question.**
  - Confidence is worked out (briefing run) governs **claims and positions**: how sure we are that something is so, or where a decision stands against its lines. Those must be computed, never typed, and a person may only lower them. The run keeps that intact: positions here (`± ⩲ = ⩱ ∓`) are read by rule, weakest line wins, and a person's only position mark is `∞`, "the records can't see this", which lowers certainty and never moves the number.
  - A `?!` is a judgement of a **choice**: was it sound, given what was known? No record can compute that. An engine can in chess because the rules are closed and every alternative can be played out; in an organisation the alternatives are a clause in a contract, a quote in a pack, an applicant market. The records can say where the decision stood; only a person can say whether the move was wise. So the prompt's "engine beside the human" holds only in part: our engine evaluates positions, never moves.
  - Where human marks would undermine worked-out confidence is if they were **averaged, scored or tallied**, or written with hindsight. So: never one number; marks are judged "as it stood" (moves to come out of focus, hindsight behind a key, a `?` needs an alternative that was on the record); two reviewers who disagree both stay, signed; and no page counts anyone's marks.
  - **This argues against `confidence-is-worked-out` only in its scope.** Its rule "never typed" should read "never typed **for claims and positions**". Judgements of choices are typed by design, by named people, in a small fixed notation, and kept apart. The disagreement between the two voices is the useful part, not a defect: the records found where the trouble **showed** (1 August, 6 October); people found where it was **made** (12 and 28 May).
- *Where reviews live, who may annotate whose work, and what stays private?*
  - **Where:** on the decision, in Mandate. A review is an annotation layer on a decision record (the minute run's record type): marks on its moves, the after-action items, the agreed date. Not a document in Artifacts (it isn't free text and must point at moves), not a new app. The book is a Mandate view over agreed reviews. A curated anthology may live in Artifacts and cite reviews by `Ref`. Questions from a review route through the Inbox.
  - **Who:** the person who made a move marks it first, on their own moves only. A reviewer from outside the moves (here an improvement lead from the Org floor) marks the rest and writes lines at critical moments. A second reviewer may add marks beside, never averaged. The meeting that made the decision agrees the review, as it agrees a minute; it never re-marks. The records find candidate moments by rule. The model, if allowed, drafts in pencil only and never marks.
  - **Private:** your marks on your own moves until you release them; the reviewer's mark is sealed from the mover until the mover releases (anti-anchoring, shown as a sealed card, not hidden); drafts are pencil for the people in the review; a learner's guesses are never stored; reading notes stay on the reader's own shelf; reading progress is the reader's. **Ours:** the agreed review, readable by whoever can open the decision. **Never:** a review of a person, a tally of anyone's marks, a search by who played a move.
- *What would AUGUR need to record during work to make replay possible?*
  - Every step as a dated event with who made it and which side it was (ours or the world's). Mandate already has most of this for decisions and actions (minute run).
  - **What was on the record by each date**: papers in the pack, facts and their values then (point-in-time reads; time as an address).
  - **The lines a decision sets itself** (revisit lines, aims), bound to saved questions, so the position can be computed as at any date.
  - **Options on the table** at a decision or a big step, with where each was recorded.
  - **One-way steps** when taken, with their way back and its cost; **what each step depends on**; **assumptions** with a date to test them; **clocks** (notice periods and deadlines from contracts).
  - Moved dates kept as history (the minute run has them).
  - For the book: **facets** on a decision when it is drafted (what it changes, what it depends on, its one-way step, trial or for good), so its position can be recognised before it is played.

**Where these push back:**
- **On the prompt's "engine evaluation bar":** there is no engine for organisational choices, and building a model that "evaluates moves" would be the typed confidence the briefing run forbade, with a model's name on it. The computed view here is deliberately dull: three lines, a rule, a sign. It can't evaluate a move, and says so.
- **On `confidence-is-worked-out`:** narrow its scope, as above. A named person's judgement of a choice is valuable precisely because it isn't worked out.
- **On reviewing only what went badly:** a book of failures teaches avoidance and survivorship. Chess collections are mostly wins. Review reversals and amendments of chosen kinds, and a sample of kept decisions (R-03 and R-11 are kept games, and they carry the book line). I'd propose: every reversal of a kind whose settings say so, and one kept decision per quarter per body.
- **On "findable by kind of situation like an opening book":** a code taxonomy without an owner rots. Lead with facets (four questions answered when a decision is drafted); codes are names for common facet combinations, owned per deployment, and optional.
- **On calm (Harry's principle 6):** play through and the step-by-step replays must only move on a key press. Here they play once as the page's moment and then wait.
- **On explain, never hide:** the reviewer's mark is sealed from the mover until she releases hers. That is a deliberate, temporary hide for fairness; it is shown as a sealed card that says who marked and when it opens. I think it passes the principle; Harry should confirm.
- **On the letterbox:** not used, rightly. Nothing in a review is urgent.

**Decisions for Harry** (none taken):
1. **Reviews as an annotation layer on Mandate decisions**, with marks on moves, after-action items and an agreed date. Recommended. Needs a record in augur-mandate (the review type) and, for the notation as a cross-app part, an AUGUR ADR.
2. **The notation**: six move marks, five positions, `∞`, four signs, glyphs fixed and words from data. Recommended. It needs a font decision: IBM Plex Mono (itself still waiting on an ADR) has `±` but not `⩲ ⩱ ∓ ∞ □ ⊕`; here they render from fallback fonts. Options: subset a maths font for the glyphs, draw them as SVG data marks, or drop to `± = ∓` plus `+=` and `=+` in plain text.
3. **Record one-way steps, dependencies, options and assumptions** on Mandate actions and decisions. Recommended now, independent of reviews. Needs a record in augur-mandate.
4. **Which decisions get a review**: every reversal or amendment of kinds that opt in, plus a sample of kept ones; a facilitator from outside the moves. Your call on the sample.
5. **Own moves first, sealed reviewer marks, no tally by person**: recommended as rules in `docs/app-patterns.md` once a review ships.
6. **The book's catalogue**: facets on every draft (four questions), codes optional and owned per deployment in Admin. Recommended.
7. **The model's part**: none needed. Recommended off for reviews; at most pencil drafts of a line, opt-in per body.
8. **Questions from disagreements** routed as Inbox "needs" with no escalation (agrees with the minute run's rule for actions).
