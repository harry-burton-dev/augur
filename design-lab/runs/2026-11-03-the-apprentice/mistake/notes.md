# Surface 6 · A mistake

**The moment:** on Friday 6 Nov at 11:20, Rhiannon Price, the harbourmaster, sees berth allocation put MV Arctic Dawn in a berth for vessels under 3,000 GT. She knows it is nearer 5,000. Survey reader had read 2,890 from a Nordhavn Class certificate: its **net** tonnage, read as gross, because older Nordhavn certificates print net first under the headings "NT | GT". Ana Ferreira had accepted it on Thursday in a batch of 52 with a sample of 8; the one Nordhavn certificate in the sample was the new layout, so it passed. Nine tonnages were made the same way since Wednesday; six are wrong. Two of them were used: in Rhiannon's Mandate intent (Winter lay-up berths) and in Jess Lindqvist's briefing (Lay-up capacity, November).

**Data:** `DATA.mistake` (the fact, why it made sense, the batch and its sample, the lot, nine siblings with old or new layout, where they were used, four review options, the condition to go back). Keys: R, T, G and those on each page.

**What every variant keeps:** the mistake is found, undone, repaired **visibly**, and the scope reviewed **by a person**. Nothing about the agent changes silently.

---

## V1: The seam
*Idea.* The vessel's record with the repair in place: 2,890 struck through, 5,140 in, a seam down the side, and a thread under it in time order: read by the agent (Thu), signed in a batch (Thu), flagged by Rhiannon (Fri 11:20), repaired by Ana (Fri 13:05). A side panel names who did what and why it made sense to the agent.

*Sources.* Visible repair and kintsugi (wear and time run); errata; conservation records.

*What works.* The seam answers "was this ever wrong, and who fixed it?" on the record itself, for good. Naming four people and one agent in order, without a word of blame, is the tone the run wants. The maker's mark stays on the struck value: the agent's mistake is attributed, not hidden.

*What fails or risks.* A record with many seams gets noisy; seams on minor corrections (a typo in a name) need a quieter form. "Never edited away" needs a policy for legal removal (personal data).

*Principles.* Visible repair: strong. Explain, never hide. Calm: the seam stitches in once.

*Needs.* A detail history in Data Atlas (proposed, accepted, disputed, repaired) with actors; `agent.repair` in the audit.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V2: Recall by lot
*Idea.* One flagged detail recalls its lot: every accepted detail made the same way (same agent, same kind, same issuer, same detail type, the runs since Wednesday). Nine cards are checked in turn: six wrong (old layout), three right (new layout). "Undo and repair the 6" does them together, each with its own seam. Until checked, the lot shows as "recalled" wherever it appears, and nothing new is made the same way.

*Sources.* Product recalls by batch and lot number; aviation airworthiness directives (one finding on one aircraft triggers checks across the fleet).

*What works.* **A mistake has siblings.** An agent's errors are systematic, not random, so the right response to one is to look at everything made the same way. The lot is defined by data AUGUR already has (agent, kind, issuer, run), and the recall turned up the pattern (old layout wrong, new right) that the review needs.

*What fails or risks.* How wide the lot is drawn is a judgement: too narrow misses siblings, too wide recalls hundreds of good facts. Here it is narrow and lucky. Recalled facts shown as "recalled" everywhere may alarm readers more than the error does.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide. Change arrives as work (a round of small checks). Calm.

*Needs.* Lot keys on every proposal (agent, kind, source issuer, detail type, run); a "recalled" state on accepted details; a batch undo that leaves a seam on each.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V3: The review
*Idea.* A short blameless review in five questions: what happened, why it made sense to the agent, what caught it, why the checks didn't, what changes. Questions 1 to 4 are counted and quoted from the records by template; question 5 is the supervisor's, as ticked changes (sample across issuers and their layouts; teach the pattern reader headings, not column order; narrow Nordhavn). "Retrain or replace the model" is offered and not ticked.

*Sources.* Just culture (Reason; Dekker); morbidity and mortality conferences; CHIRP, the confidential aviation reporting programme.

*What works.* Question 4 is the most valuable line in the run: **two readers with the same wrong idea agree**, and **a random sample can miss a systematic error**. Both are lessons about the checks, not the agent, and both lead to concrete settings (stratify by layout; make the pattern reader independent). "No one is blamed" is said and meant: not the agent, not the person who signed the sample AUGUR drew.

*What fails or risks.* Writing a review for every mistake is too much; it should be triggered by a recall above a size, or by use downstream. "Why it made sense" relies on the workings being honest; a model's after-the-fact explanation is not its method (keep it to the recorded steps).

*Principles.* The workings show; pencil and ink (1–4 template, 5 a person). Calm.

*Needs.* A review object linked to a recall; sampling settings (strata) that the review can change.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V4: What the undo touches
*Idea.* Before the repair goes in, the places the wrong numbers went: the intent (Rhiannon, 9 following) and the briefing (Jess, 58 readers, with two wrong vessels in it). The correction each owner will receive is drafted from the records: what was wrong, what it is, how it happened, and "your briefing isn't changed: you decide". The supervisor sends it, not the agent: telling people is on its ceiling.

*Sources.* Newspaper corrections columns; recall notices; consequence preview and reach before act (earlier runs).

*What works.* It makes the cost of a mistake visible where it lands, and it keeps ownership right: the repair changes the facts, the owners decide about their own documents. Counting reach (2 owners told directly, 67 see a correction where they read it) keeps the correction proportionate.

*What fails or risks.* Downstream tracing depends on Mandate bindings and briefings citing details by reference; a number copied into prose is lost. A correction notice for every repair would be noise; it should go only where the fact was used.

*Principles.* Reach before act; consequence preview; explain, never hide.

*Needs.* Data Atlas events for "a detail you used has been repaired" (Mandate already follows bindings through events); Artifacts briefings that keep their query, not their numbers (the briefing run).

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V5: The scope review
*Idea.* After the recall, four options for the supervisor, each with its cost: keep the level (with the sample drawn across layouts), narrow Nordhavn Class to "asks each time" (+6 checks a week), narrow all details (+230 a week), or pause. The rungs of every kind are shown as they would be. AUGUR's suggestion (the narrowest change that covers what failed) is in pencil and never applied. The reason is pre-filled from the recall by template; the condition to go back is stated (20 Nordhavn details accepted unchanged in a row, then a person decides).

*Sources.* Clinical supervision after an incident (proportionate, recorded, reviewed); the run's own ladder and caps.

*What works.* **Narrow only what failed.** The option costs make it obvious: narrowing everything would cost 230 checks a week to fix a problem in 6. The way back is written at the moment of narrowing, so trust isn't lost for good by one bad week.

*What fails or risks.* A supervisor under pressure may pick "pause" to feel safe; the costs need to be as visible as the risk. "20 in a row" is arbitrary; it should come from the sampling table.

*Principles.* Trust is a person's decision; consequence preview; pencil for AUGUR's suggestion.

*Needs.* Grant changes per kind with a reason and a "back when" condition; the review option set as a kit part.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 4

## V6: The andon cord
*Idea.* Rhiannon, who doesn't supervise Survey reader, sees the wrong tonnage in her intent. She can pull a cord that stops **that kind of work** (details from Nordhavn Class certificates) at once. It is reversible, never undoes anything, carries her name, shows in the agent's mode line (Next: Hold), and lands in Ana's Inbox. Only the supervisor restarts, narrows or keeps it stopped.

*Sources.* Toyota's andon cord and jidoka (anyone on the line can stop it when they see a defect; the team lead decides what next).

*What works.* It gives **everyone affected** a way to act on an agent's mistake without giving them control of the agent. Stopping one kind of work is the smallest safe action: work waits, nothing is lost. Pulling it is cheap, so people will use it early, which is the point.

*What fails or risks.* A cord anyone can pull can be pulled for the wrong reasons (a disagreement about a fact, not a mistake). Each pull must carry a reason and be visible to the supervisor, and repeated pulls by one person may need a conversation. It needs Harry's decision: it widens who can affect an agent beyond its supervisor.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: the stop is visible everywhere the agent shows. Calm: one stop, one notice.

*Needs.* A `paused` flag per kind of work with who, when and why, open to anyone who can read the work; the mode line showing it.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## Ranking
1. **V2 Recall by lot**: a mistake has siblings; check everything made the same way.
2. **V6 The andon cord**: anyone affected can stop one kind of work at once.
3. **V1 The seam**: the repair stays on the record, with the chain named.
4. **V3 The review**: the checks' blind spots, written down without blame.
5. **V5 The scope review**: narrow only what failed, with the way back.
6. **V4 What the undo touches**: corrections where the numbers landed.

**What I'd build first.** Lot keys on every agent proposal and a recall that checks the lot together, repairing each with a seam (V2 and V1); the cord (V6) for anyone who can see the work, pausing one kind; the review's question 4 as a standing check on sampling (stratify by the source's layout as well as its issuer). The scope review (V5) is the existing rung page opened from the recall.
