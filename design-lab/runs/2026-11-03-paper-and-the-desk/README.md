This is the batch 1 wildcard, W1, "paper and the desk". The cinematic run and batch 1 looked at screens through other screens (film, instruments, maps). This run looks instead at the **paper tools** people still trust for high-stakes work, and asks what AUGUR gains by behaving like a well-run desk: work you can pick up and hand over.

Sources:
- flight progress strips and the move to electronic strips (MacKay, *Is paper safer?*, 1999; NATS EFPS);
- ward boards and clinical handover (Wears and Perry 2007; SBAR; I-PASS, Starmer et al. 2014);
- T-card boards and Toyota's kanban, heijunka and kamishibai;
- double-entry ledgers and good documentation practice (ALCOA+);
- Luhmann's Zettelkasten;
- ringi-sho and hanko;
- railway token working;
- Sellen and Harper's *The Myth of the Paperless Office*.

The run has 40 prototypes. There are six surfaces of six variants each:
- the desk language;
- Home as a strip bay;
- approvals;
- handover;
- the team board;
- corrections and audit.

Four combined screens complete it. All sample data is one housing association: Amara Osei, a repairs coordinator on the Repairs North team, is "you". Each page plays its moment about 1.2 s after load, and **R** replays it. Every page lists its other keys at the top right.

As a wildcard, this run **argues against one existing concept**. Focal planes are right for one person's focus, but wrong for work that is handed between people: a neighbour's bay must stay sharp, or a handover to you can sit in the blur. See [Ink and light](grammar/v5-ink-and-light.html).

Start with [The handover, both sides](combined/v1-handover-both-sides.html) and [The bay and the stamp](combined/v2-the-bay-and-the-stamp.html). The prompt is in `prompt.md`, the ranking in `SHORTLIST.md` and the new concepts in `concepts.md`.
