# Surface 3 · The working set

**The moment:** the context assembled for one task (WS-31, the Brightwell renewal decision), as an object a person can review, prune and hand over. It has 14 pieces (5 data, 5 information, 4 context) and 3 set aside. Each piece carries its rung, source and floor, what lifted it, why it is here in its author's words, who it is for, and what it holds while.

**Keys:** R replays, T shows the admin words. Each page lists its own keys in the top bar.

---

## V1: The bundle index
*Idea.* The working set as a court bundle with an index. Each piece gets a tab number, its rung, its source (app, record, floor, and "lifted from tabs 1, 2"), why it is here (quoted, with name and date) and what it holds while. Context comes first, then information, then data. **Set aside** sits below with its reasons, kept and never deleted. At the moment, Tom sets aside the complaints count with a required reason. The page warns which tabs were lifted from it, and the row moves down rather than out.

*Sources.* Court bundle index (tabbed, paginated, agreed between parties); archival finding aids.

*What works.* It is the plainest form of the object, and it answers "can I hand this over?" A stranger can read every reason without asking. "Set aside, with why, so nobody adds them back without knowing" is the pruning rule. "Every piece is a link to its record, never a copy" (copies know their source) keeps the set honest when records change.

*What fails or risks.* A table of 14 rows with six columns is dense. The reason column wraps badly at 1280, so the rung column is dropped there (the group headers carry it). It doesn't show how pieces depend on each other, beyond the "lifted from tabs" line.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: one move, then still. No letterbox.

*Needs.* The working-set piece record (see the shortlist). A required reason on set-aside.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 4

## V2: The release
*Idea.* An airline dispatch release: every piece has a validity window from when it was added until its condition is expected to break. It is drawn on one calendar with *today* and *the decision* marked. A piece that runs out before the decision is flagged: the renewal offer is valid 30 days, until 19 Nov. The release needs two names: **assembled by** Tom, who answers for what is in it, and **released by** Grace, the named person with release authority for the intent.

*Sources.* Airline dispatch release (dispatcher and captain both sign; weather and NOTAMs valid for a period); TAF validity periods.

*What works.* "Runs out before the decision" is the cheapest staleness check there is, and it fires *before* anything goes stale. The two signatures put the principle "every output has a named person with release authority" into the object. The validity bars make "holds while" visible at a glance.

*What fails or risks.* Most conditions have no end date ("holds while Brightwell keeps its definition"), so most bars fade out to open and say little. A release can suggest that the decider signs off the set's contents, when she signs that she is content to decide on it.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: good. Calm: one flag. No letterbox, though a set running out before a decision would be a candidate if the decision were urgent.

*Needs.* `validUntil` where a source has one (offers, permits, contracts). A release signature on the working set.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V3: Questions first
*Idea.* The decider's questions come first (set by Grace on 30 Oct, plus one Tom added). Every piece is filed under the question it answers. A question with only something said under it ("Can Brightwell fix it, and how fast?") is a gap with a name: who could answer it (Kerry Lund at Brightwell), with what, and an *Ask* button. Coverage bars show which rungs each answer reaches. **A piece that answers no question can't be added** without a new question, so the set can't grow by habit.

*Sources.* Commander's critical information requirements and priority intelligence requirements (the commander states what she needs to know before anyone collects); the reference desk's "the question is the record".

*What works.* It is the best answer to "how big should a working set be?": as big as its questions, no bigger. The decider owns the questions and the assembler fills them, which makes the split between owner and decider clear. "4 of 6 answered well enough to decide" is an honest status.

*What fails or risks.* The decider must set the questions up front, and many won't. The first questions may come from the assembler and get confirmed later. A piece can answer several questions and appears under each, which repeats it.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong (an unanswered question is drawn with a person). Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* Questions as part of a Mandate decision or the working set; a piece-to-question link.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V4: Found, screened, kept
*Idea.* How the set was assembled, as a systematic review's flow diagram. 23 found (in Data Atlas, Artifacts and Mandate, with the search used in each), 4 found twice, 19 screened, 5 set aside (each reason counted), 14 kept by rung. The sum is shown and checked ("23 − 4 − 5 = 14 ✓"). Click a box to list what is in it.

*Sources.* The PRISMA flow diagram for systematic reviews; the answer has a base (concept).

*What works.* It shows *where nobody looked*, which no other variant does: the searches are named, so a reviewer can say "you didn't look in the round log". Counts that add up mean nothing drops out unseen. It is good for an agent's assembly too: the agent's searches would show here.

*What fails or risks.* Most people assemble context by memory and links, not by searching, so the "found" box would often be fiction. It is a process view, useful in review and dull every day.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* Search provenance when pieces are added from search; set-aside reasons as a short list plus free text.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 3

## V5: Where used
*Idea.* The set as a bill of materials: the decision question at the top, context under it, then the information and readings each rests on. Pick a reading and every piece above it lights (*27 missed collections in October* holds up 5 pieces, one of them context for the decision). Readings used by nothing are marked "on its own".

*Sources.* The indented bill of materials and where-used report from manufacturing; the recipe is the record.

*What works.* It finds the load-bearing readings: if *27* is wrong, five pieces fall. That is what a new owner needs to check first, and what a staleness check should watch hardest. "Used in nothing · on its own" is a gentle pruning hint.

*What fails or risks.* The tree repeats a reading under every piece that uses it (27 appears four times). Context pieces that rest only on documents show as leaves, which undersells them. It is good as a mode, not as a home view.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: good. Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* `from` links between pieces, which the lift record already implies.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V6: The agent's bundle
*Idea.* What an agent was given, as a jury is given a bundle. Each document comes with a **direction** on what it may be used for ("Only for how long the alternatives take"). The **not given** list shows each item hatched, with why ("Said, not checked: a person weighs it"). What it said back is listed in pencil with its maker's mark, then accepted (it becomes piece 13 with the mark kept) or not (struck, with Tom's reason). The header says whom it works under, its model calls, and when the bundle was sealed and ends.

*Sources.* Jury bundles and judicial directions on the use of evidence; the apprentice run's works under someone, maker's mark and pencil and ink.

*What works.* It answers the prompt's agent question directly: **a person can review what the agent was given, not only what it said.** Directions turn "access" into "purpose", in line with the label run's *purpose is the key*. The not-given list is as important as the given one: it is the agent's honest blank.

*What fails or risks.* Directions are free text, and the agent can't be held to them by anything but review. Three documents is easy; thirty would need grouping. The page hints at a check (did it use only what it was given?) without doing it. Combined V4 does it.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* A grant scoped to a working set and an end date (the apprentice's permits). Direction text per piece. `proposedBy` / `acceptedBy` (apprentice).

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4
