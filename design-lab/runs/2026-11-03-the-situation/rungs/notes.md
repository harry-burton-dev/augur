# Surface 2 · One reading, three rungs

**The moment:** *27 missed collections in October* is raised from data to information to context, step by step. Each variant takes a different view of how a person sees the climb and what lifts it.

**Shared data:** as in the ladder notes. The reading comes from Data Atlas (missed collection reports, kept on the Team floor by Mira Haddad). The comparisons come from an Explorer query. The threshold is clause 7.2 of the Brightwell contract (more than 20 missed in a month, three months running, is a service failure). The context is Grace Obi's renewal decision by 1 Dec.

**Keys:** R replays, T shows the admin words. Each page has its own keys for stepping, listed in its top bar.

---

## V1: One sentence that climbs
*Idea.* Stretchtext: one sentence grows a clause per lift. *27 missed collections in October* · *, twice July's 13* · *, and over the contract's limit of 20 for the second month running.* · *For Grace's renewal decision on 1 Dec, that matters:* · *one more month over makes it a service failure…* · *Brightwell's own count is 19…*. The rung mark on the left climbs as the sentence does. The right column says what each clause adds and who chose it (template, or a person).

*Sources.* Ted Nelson's stretchtext; Ackoff; the nut graf.

*What works.* The climb is something you can read, and it shows in one place that rungs one and two can be built by template while rung three starts when a person names the question. Taking a clause away drops the sentence back a rung, never to nothing. ← and → step through it.

*What fails or risks.* The finished sentence is long (64 words), well past the briefing's one-minute budget for a headline. At the context rung the sentence belongs to one reader, so for three readers it has to be written three times.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: good (every clause names its source of meaning). Calm: the sentence grows once. No letterbox.

*Needs.* Sentence templates per lift kind; a reader slot.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V2: The nut graf
*Idea.* The working set as a news story: the lede (information), the nut graf (*"Why it matters to you…"*, the context paragraph for one reader, lit in her floor colour), the other side (where sources and frames disagree), what's next (the projection) and what we don't know (gaps, with who could fill them). A margin names each paragraph's job and its rung. Press N to take the nut graf out: everything else stays true, and it reads as information for nobody in particular.

*Sources.* The newspaper nut graf and "why it matters" (Axios, *The Wall Street Journal*'s "nut graf"); the inverted pyramid.

*What works.* N is the best demonstration in the run that **context is one paragraph with a reader, added to information that belongs to everyone.** The story's built-in "other side" and "what we don't know" make disagreement and gaps a normal part of the page, not an alarm. Every sentence is built from the set's pieces, so the numbers can't drift.

*What fails or risks.* News form invites spin: a model would love to write the lede. It must stay template-built, with the nut graf signed by a person. The margin labels cost a column of width.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* Paragraph templates; a signed nut-graf slot on the working set (one per reader).

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V3: From observation to warning
*Idea.* Impact-based weather warnings. The observation is data. The forecast ("Realistic possibility that November is over 20 too", with the yardstick word worked out by rule) is information, the same for everyone. The **warning** is context: the forecast placed on an impact × likelihood matrix *per reader*. Grace gets "Take action", Mira "Be aware" and the Org "No warning: for information".

*Sources.* The Met Office's impact-based warnings matrix; Endsley's level 3 (projection); the briefing's probability yardstick.

*What works.* It is the cleanest picture of "same information, different context": one likelihood, three impacts, three warnings. Weather warnings are familiar, so people read it quickly. The Org's "no warning" makes quiet a real result, not a missing one.

*What fails or risks.* Impact per reader is a judgement, and it has to be set by someone, per kind of decision. The amber and red tints come close to the alert colours the shell keeps for urgency: a warning level here must never look like a shell alert. That conflicts with the cinematic run's rule that the shell owns loudness. A 4×4 matrix is a lot for one figure.

*Principles.* Calm: risky (warning words). Explain, never hide: good. Letterbox: not used, and it must not be.

*Needs.* Impact per reader per kind of decision. A rule that a working-set warning never raises a shell notice.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 2

## V4: Authentic, probative, of consequence
*Idea.* The rules of evidence as the three rungs. *Is it what it says it is?* (authentic data). *Does it make something more or less likely?* (information). *Is that of consequence to this decision?* (context). A piece climbs one rung per test it passes and stops at the first it fails. The rate per 1,000 passes two and fails the third, so it is set aside with why. The supplier's driver story is admitted **for the saying, not the truth**, as hearsay is.

*Sources.* Rules of evidence on relevance (probative and of consequence) and hearsay (a statement admitted only for the fact it was said); Wigmore.

*What works.* The three questions are the most usable test in the run for whether something belongs in a working set, and they give "set aside" a reason anyone can check. "Admitted for the saying, not the truth" settles how to hold the said-not-checked pieces: kept, marked, not counted as fact.

*What fails or risks.* A table of tests is a reviewer's view, too slow for assembling. "Probative" needs plain words (the page uses them). Courts have judges, and here the person assembling is also the judge.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* Nothing new beyond the reason field and a "said, not checked" flag.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 3

## V5: Take a lift away
*Idea.* The climb as a recipe of steps (read; compare with July; hold against clause 7.2; split by round; ask it of the renewal; say what happens next; add the offer). Each step has a switch. Turn one off and the sentence, the rung and a "what fell" box update: without the question, it is true and for nobody, and the offer now holds nothing up. A step whose input is off says "holds nothing up now".

*Sources.* Power Query's applied steps; the recipe is the record (concept); Frické.

*What works.* It is the only variant where you can test what each lift contributes, which is exactly how a reviewer should check a set ("what if we drop the projection?"). Dependencies between lifts show without a diagram. The first step can't be turned off, because the reading is always there.

*What fails or risks.* Switches suggest the steps are independent and can be reordered, but most aren't. It is a tool for checking, not for reading.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong (off steps stay visible, dashed). Calm. No letterbox.

*Needs.* A dependency between lifts ("projection needs threshold"). The recipe stored with the piece.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V6: Focus pull
*Idea.* Three focal planes: the reading near, its meaning (the chart with the limit) in the middle, its situation (the question, clause 7.2, the offer, the alternatives, the calendar to 31 Jan) far back. Each lift racks focus back one plane, and a lens scale at the corner says which rung is sharp and what pulled it.

*Sources.* Rack focus and deep focus; the cinematic run's focal planes.

*What works.* It looks like AUGUR: planes, blur and quiet, all in the water language. It gives a felt sense that context is *wider* than information, not just higher. Focus moves without anything else moving.

*What fails or risks.* Blur hides text, so the rung that isn't in focus can't be read, and the cinematic run found that blur reads as "disabled". Three overlapping panes at 1280 is crowded. It is a demonstration, not a working view.

*Principles.* Focus stacking: strong. Explain, never hide: weak (blurred planes are unreadable). Calm: good.

*Needs.* `--x-proposed-far-blur` (carried from the cinematic run).

*Scores.* Clarity 3 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## Ranked
1. **V2 The nut graf**: context as one signed paragraph with a reader.
2. **V1 One sentence that climbs**: the climb you can read.
3. **V5 Take a lift away**: the reviewer's check.
4. **V4 Authentic, probative, of consequence**: the test for belonging.
5. **V6 Focus pull**: right feel, wrong for reading.
6. **V3 Observation to warning**: right idea, wrong loudness.

**What I'd build first.** V2's shape for how a working set reads to its decider: shared paragraphs built from pieces by template, plus **one nut graf per reader, signed by a person**. Under it, V4's three questions as the help on "Add a piece" (is it what it says? does it move a likelihood? does it bear on this decision?), and V5's "take a lift away" as the review mode.
