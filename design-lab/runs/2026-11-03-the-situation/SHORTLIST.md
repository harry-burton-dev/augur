**Answers to the prompt's questions:**
- *Is a working set a kind of Artifacts document, a Data Atlas query, a Mandate attachment, or its own object? Who owns it?*
  - **Its own object, stored as a kind of Artifacts document.** It needs what Artifacts already gives a document: versions that never change once issued, a library whose floor sets who can read it, and withdrawal. It holds nothing but links, a few fields per piece, the decision's questions, frames and the set-aside list.
  - **It is not a Data Atlas query.** A query can't hold a reason, a reader, a frame or something said and not checked. Queries are *pieces* (an Explorer query is the usual information piece).
  - **It is not a Mandate attachment.** One set can serve several intents and outlive them. Mandate *links* to it, and a decision names the version it rests on.
  - **Ownership.** It has exactly one **owner**, the person who assembled it or took it over by readback (someone holds it). It also has a **releaser**: the named person with release authority for the decision, who signs that she is content to decide on this version. Owner and releaser can be the same person, but the page always shows both roles. The pieces keep their own access: a reader who can open the set but not a piece sees that piece hatched, with whom to ask.
- *What a working set made for an agent must contain, so a person can review what the agent was given, and how this agrees with the apprentice.*
  1. The question it was asked, word for word.
  2. Each **given** piece, the same record as a person's set, plus a **direction**: what it may be used for.
  3. Each **not given** item that was considered, with why (said, not checked; personal data; not its job).
  4. Its supervisor and deputy, and the grant's end date (the task's decision date).
  5. Its model calls against its limit.
  6. What it said back, in pencil with its maker's mark. **Every clause cites a given piece and place.** A clause that cites anything outside the bundle, or nothing, is held for a person ("a guess or a leak").
  7. After acceptance, the clause becomes a piece with its mark kept and "accepted by…".

  This agrees with the apprentice point for point: it works under someone, its work is pencil until a person accepts it, the mark is kept, the mode line says what it is doing now, and there is no supervisor, no run. When the set is handed over, the agent's supervision goes with it and the agent waits until the new owner reads back. It adds two rules: **the bundle is the agent's whole world** (outside citations are held), and **an agent may lift by rule and propose a lift by judgement, but never sign one**. I'd add "lift a piece to context" and "release a working set" to the apprentice's ceiling.

  Six fields are new and belong to the working set alone: rung, lift, why here, holds while, answers and state. The events service needs four kinds of event to make "holds while" work: a document withdrawn or superseded (Artifacts), a fact or count revised (Data Atlas), a person moving post (Identity), and a date passing (the clock the briefing run already asked for).

**Where these push back:**
- **"Context goes stale: it says when it was assembled."** A date alone is the wrong signal. It invites people to distrust old pieces that still hold (the July count) and to trust new ones whose condition already broke. Show the date, but stale means a broken condition.
- **"Assembled from what AUGUR holds."** AUGUR can suggest pieces and lift by rule. It must never assemble context on its own, because context needs a reader and a reason, and those are a person's. An agent's set is pencil until a person accepts each piece.
- **"Relative to someone" can get loud.** Most readers of most information should not be told at all. Per-reader context should be quiet by default: a reader is told only if it changes what they would do.
- **Showing both frames is for two people, not for the decider.** The decider needs the agreed facts and the issues, each with what would decide it, not two essays side by side.
- **Explain, never hide, against calm.** Changes that don't break a load-bearing piece (the route revert, the revised count) are repaired in place with a seam and no notice. Only a broken condition under a piece the decision rests on tells anyone, and only its owner, once.
- **The DIKW pyramid.** The run uses its three rungs as words and drops "knowledge" and "wisdom". It treats each climb as an act someone signs, not a filter.

**Decisions for Harry** (none taken):
1. Make the working set a new Artifacts document type, with its own schema, linking into Data Atlas, Explorer and Mandate? I recommend yes. It needs a Proposed record.
2. Separate owner and releaser on every working set, both always shown, with the releaser being whoever has release authority for the decision? I recommend yes. The same person may hold both.
3. Make "holds while" required? I recommend required for information and context. For data it defaults to "the record isn't revised or withdrawn", which Data Atlas can watch.
4. Put the four events on the events service roadmap (document withdrawn or superseded, fact revised, person moved post, a date passing)? Stale context depends on them.
5. Add "lift to context" and "release a working set" to the apprentice's ceiling, and make "held: cites outside its bundle" a standard check on agent output? I recommend both.
6. Who is told when a piece trips: only its owner, plus the releaser only if the piece is one the released decision rests on? I recommend that, and nobody else.
7. Are frames pieces of a working set (my recommendation) or Mandate discussion?
8. Make the handover question ("did anything happen that isn't in the set?") part of every handover, alongside the readback from paper and the desk? I recommend yes.
