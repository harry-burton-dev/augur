# Surface 3 · Not for you

**The moment:** an operator meets something they can't open. Owen Clarke (Commercial analyst) finds the Marlow Star record in search; Sam Ito (VTS officer) opens the initial report through the record; Leah Morgan searches; Kofi Mensah wants to use the record for a pilot's appraisal. Every variant must say that the thing exists (when they may know), why not, who can change it, and, where it applies, when it opens.

**Keys:** R, T, S as everywhere; each page lists its own.

---

## V1: The closed file
*Idea.* A record you can't open is a closed file cover in the registry: its title, its marking, why it is closed to you ("kept by Marine Operations; your floor doesn't reach it"), whose hand opens it (Ruth Okoye, keeper) and what you can say about it. What you already hold from it (the tug's charge, kept by Commercial) is listed as yours. Ask (A) opens a purpose picker that offers only uses the record's labels allow; the rest stay listed with why, and uses allowed for the record but not for the values inside say so ("the pilot's name and hours inside are kept back for this").

*Sources.* Civil service registry file covers; GSC handling instructions.

*What works.* The cover answers all four questions without a click. Asking for a purpose, not for "access", is the core idea of the run: a share is for a use, until a date.

*What fails or risks.* The hatched cover is heavy for something you meet often in search; in a list it should be a row (V3). "Yours already" can surprise: a person may not know their floor holds part of someone else's record.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: yes.

*Needs.* Shares that carry a purpose and an end date (identity and Data Atlas); the label check at request time.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V2: Redacted, with reasons
*Idea.* The report as Sam can read it: what he may not read is taken out as a bar of **fixed length** (so its size says nothing), coded in the margin by the label that withheld it (O-S·P, O-S·C), with the plain reason and who could lift it in a side panel. The pilot's name is not redacted but said another way ("a pilot"), and that is marked too.

*Sources.* Freedom of Information redaction practice (exemption codes in the margin, e.g. s.40 personal information); GSC portion marking.

*What works.* Everyone knows the redacted page; it needs no teaching. Fixed-length bars are a detail most redaction gets wrong. The difference between "withheld" and "said another way" is clear.

*What fails or risks.* Black bars are a cliché with a hostile tone, the opposite of "here's how to get it". Redacting inside prose only works where values were inserted as values (record V4). Codes in the margin are noise for operators; the side panel carries the meaning.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: moderate (the bars are loud).

*Needs.* Portion-level access on Artifacts documents (reading a document with some values withheld).

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 3

## V3: Open, locked, not told
*Idea.* In search results, three tiers with one rule each. **Open**: yours, including values said another way. **Locked**: you may know it exists; it stays in its place, hatched, with why and whom to ask, and many locked results from one keeper fold into one line with one ask (G). **Not told**: when even existence would cause harm (the policy names which descriptors, here PORT SECURITY), it is left out with no mark, and so that no list gives it away, **every list ends with the same line**, whether anything was left out or not.

*Sources.* Six ways to be empty (catalogue run), not yours to see; the Glomar response ("neither confirm nor deny"); GSC need-to-know.

*What works.* It is the only honest way to handle hidden existence: a general, always-present sentence instead of a count. Folding locked results by keeper turns ten dead ends into one request.

*What fails or risks.* The always-present line becomes wallpaper; it should be quiet and fixed in place, not repeated in every card. Folding hides which results were locked unless expanded.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: as far as it can go without harm, and it says where it stops. Calm: yes.

*Needs.* Per-descriptor "hide existence" in the policy; search that filters before counting (counts must not leak hidden items).

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V4: Four kinds of answer
*Idea.* "Not for you" depends on what for. Kofi wants to use the record for the winter rota review, then for Dafydd's annual appraisal (the moment). Every value answers in one of four ways: **you may**; **ask someone, by name** (the label allows it; one person decides); **only the person it's about** (consent can be widened only by whoever gave it); **no one can** (the policy forbids it, the clause is quoted and the policy's owner named, "so the rule can be argued with, not the clerk").

*Sources.* Creative Commons deed; just-culture reporting (CHIRP Maritime, aviation safety reporting); Kantara consent receipts.

*What works.* The most useful distinction in the run: "ask Marcus" and "no one can" are different answers and need different next steps. It also shows the just-culture rule doing its job: the line manager cannot use safety data for appraisal, and is told whose policy that is.

*What fails or risks.* A purpose picker in front of every use is friction; it belongs at the moment of sharing, exporting or feeding a model, not reading. "Appraisal" can't be detected by AUGUR: the purpose is declared, so this is show-and-record, not enforcement (see SHORTLIST).

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: one swap per purpose.

*Needs.* Uses on every label; a declared purpose on share, export and model calls.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V5: Closed until
*Idea.* "Not for you" has a date. A catalogue of what Sam can't open, each with when it opens to him and when it goes for everyone, on one time axis. The briefing is under **embargo**: the Safety committee reads it first, and it opens to Marine Operations on 12 Nov. The hours reach him inside counts now; the tug's charge never; the radar sector's label is reviewed in January.

*Sources.* The National Archives' closure periods ("closed until"); press embargoes; retention schedules.

*What works.* An embargo is a date, not a no, and saying so removes most requests. The axis puts retention in front of operators for the first time: "removed for everyone, about the end of 2032".

*What fails or risks.* Many keep rules have no fixed date ("6 years after the investigation closes"), so the bars fade; honest but vague. A ten-year axis squashes the near term, which is what operators care about.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: yes. Time as an address (wear and time run).

*Needs.* Embargo on shares (Artifacts); keep dates resolved where the rule allows.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V6: Ask in a sentence
*Idea.* The request is a sentence: "Ask ⟨Kofi Mensah⟩ to let me see ⟨the pilot's time on duty⟩ for ⟨a safety review⟩, until ⟨31 Dec 2026⟩." Whom to ask is worked out from the label (the keeper, or for consent the person it is about), never chosen. The purpose is checked as you choose (moving to "appraisal" strikes it: "No one can grant this. §6 Just culture…"). The keeper's card is shown as he will get it, already checked, with three answers: yes until a date, yes as a count, no with why.

*Sources.* Said in a sentence (catalogue run); Kantara consent receipts; the briefing run's order in a sentence.

*What works.* The ask can't be wrong: no picking the wrong person, no asking for what no one can give. "Yes, as a count" is a third answer keepers need.

*What fails or risks.* Sentence templates must be translated whole per deployment. The struck purpose can feel like a scolding; the reason must stay neutral.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Reach before act: the card shows reach (1 person).

*Needs.* A request object with purpose and end date, routed by label; the three-answer approval in the shell's approvals.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

---

## Ranking
1. **V4 Four kinds of answer**: the distinction that makes "not for you" useful.
2. **V3 Open, locked, not told**: the tiers and the always-present line.
3. **V6 Ask in a sentence**: whom to ask is worked out; the purpose is checked.
4. **V5 Closed until**: embargo and retention as dates.
5. **V1 The closed file**: the record-level cover.
6. **V2 Redacted, with reasons**.

**What I'd build first.** Every locked slot says one of four kinds of answer (V4) with a name or a clause. Lists keep locked items in place, fold them by keeper, and end with the fixed "not told" line (V3). Asking is V6's sentence, and an embargo or a closure date is shown when there is one (V5).
