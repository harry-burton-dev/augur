# Shortlist: the label

The headline: **a label is policy data on every value; AUGUR enforces what it can see happen and records the purpose for what it can't; when values meet, the strictest part wins and says so, and a value is never lost on the way, only said another way by a rule that names itself.**

**Answers to the prompt's questions:**
- *Which labels must AUGUR enforce, and which only show?* **Enforce what AUGUR can see happen; for the rest, ask the purpose, record it, show it to the keeper.**
  - Enforced:
    - the marking decides who can open a value (floor plus need-to-know: OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE goes to named people or groups, never a whole floor);
    - caveats decide how a value renders for each reader (said another way, inside counts only, withheld), on the server;
    - the uses that are AUGUR's own acts: **model use** (the model gateway refuses a labelled value), **outside** (the letterbox, an approver and a lawful basis), **reporting as a count** (counts made by Explorer over the policy's minimum group);
    - **keep until** (removal in place wherever a copy is tracked);
    - **consent withdrawal**;
    - **hidden existence** (filtered before anything is counted);
    - custody is always recorded and can't be turned off.
  - Shown and recorded, not enforced:
    - purposes AUGUR can't observe (appraisal, operations versus safety). They are declared on the share or request, checked against the label, kept on the receipt and shown to the keeper;
    - origin and grade;
    - copying out of the browser, which AUGUR can help with (a marked copy) but not prevent.
- *How does a label combine when values with different labels meet?*
  - **The strictest marking wins, and it says which part raised it and the way down**: remove it, quote it as a count, or quote it as a link.
  - Descriptors add up. There is one exception, and it is a rule with an owner: a PERSONAL value said as a count over the minimum group, picking out no one, drops PERSONAL. It stays OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE, and the chip says "−PERSONAL by rule 3".
  - Uses narrow to what every part allows.
  - Each part keeps its own keep-until, so a paper outlives a value quoted in it, and the place where the value was says it was removed.
  - Caveats travel with their part and apply per reader.
  - An unlabelled value is handled as OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE until someone labels it. A suggested label is pencil until a person inks it.
  - Anyone may make a label stricter. Only its descriptor's owner may make it less strict, and that leaves a seam.
- *What would each app need to send and keep that it doesn't today?*
  - **Admin**:
    - the handling policy as data: markings, descriptors with owners and "hide existence", uses, keep rules, clauses, the phrase list, the minimum group, the default for unlabelled values;
    - need-to-know groups. **ADR: labels and the handling policy as AUGUR data** (where they live: registry or a new service).
  - **Gateway and identity**:
    - grants with a purpose and an end date;
    - group audiences;
    - a label check on every read and share, with "render as reader" on the server. **ADR: attribute-based access at the gateway** (it changes how every app is called).
  - **Data Atlas**:
    - a label and a caveat per fact, and "about" links to people;
    - consent links;
    - a count-only query path that enforces the minimum group;
    - a collection label computed from its parts, with expected counts and coverage notes.
  - **Artifacts**:
    - a portion label per paragraph, and quotes as labelled inline values (transclusion) instead of pasted text;
    - a banner worked out from the parts;
    - embargoes, share receipts, and removal in place.
  - **Explorer**:
    - answers that keep their parts' labels and the combine result;
    - the count transform;
    - never sending a labelled value to the model gateway.
  - **Model gateway**: refuses values whose label says "model use: may not", and records the refusal.
  - **Events service**:
    - custody events in one shape across apps (hand, act, what, where, to, purpose, receipt, label, transform);
    - reads aggregated above the minimum group;
    - a "hand-over not seen" event;
    - **a clock**, so a keep-until or a share's end becomes an event (the briefing run asked for the same clock). **ADR: custody events as domain events**, including the read-privacy rule.
  - **A copies index** (which quote or count came from which value), so retention, withdrawal and corrections reach every copy. **ADR**, because it crosses Data Atlas, Artifacts and Explorer.

**Where these push back on Harry's principles:**
- **Explain, never hide has a limit.** For a few descriptors (here PORT SECURITY), even saying a thing exists does harm. The run's answer is one fixed sentence on every record and every list, whether anything was left out or not, so no single page gives it away. That is a deliberate exception and needs Harry's acceptance.
- **"Read in a second" fits three parts, not six.** The badge carries the marking, the uses as symbols and the keep date. Origin, caveat and custody live in the deed, one click away.
- **Food labels use colour; AUGUR shouldn't.** The label is carried by type weight and rule (a hairline, a double rule) so it never competes with the floor colours or the state colours.
- **Strictest wins over-classifies.** The answer is to name the cause, offer the way down, and offer "quote as a link". It isn't to soften the rule.
- **AUGUR can't stop text leaving the browser.** The edge ([record V6](record/v6-the-edge.html)) is help, not enforcement, and must be described that way.
- **Re-identification in free text can't be caught reliably.** ¶4 was stopped because it was built by quoting the record and the answer. Typed as plain words, it would have gone through. Quoting values instead of typing them is the only real defence.

**Decisions for Harry** (none taken):
1. Accept the enforce-or-record line above as the rule for every app? I recommend yes.
2. Accept the "not told" exception to explain, never hide, with the fixed sentence on every record and list? I recommend yes, limited to descriptors the policy marks "hide existence".
3. Minimum group for counts and for showing reads: a policy setting, with what default? The sample uses 3. I recommend a default of 5, which is common in statistical disclosure practice, with each deployment free to change it.
4. Others' reads are counted, never named. Should the data protection officer have a break-glass view of names, which itself leaves a custody entry? I recommend yes.
5. The letterbox for leaving the organisation: every outside share, or the first per recipient plus any OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE? I recommend the second.
6. Upstream notes to keepers ("your values went into ¶3"): quiet custody entries, or notices? I recommend custody entries, with a notice only for OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE and above.
7. "About me" (custody V4): self-service for every person, or a view the data protection officer runs? I recommend starting with the officer's view.
8. Should AUGUR ship a starter policy (GSC-shaped for public bodies, ISO-shaped for companies)? I recommend shipping both as files a deployment copies. Neither belongs in code.
9. Labels carried by type and rule only, with no colour? I recommend yes.
