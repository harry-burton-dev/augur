# Shortlist: the intent

Ranked by what I'd build first. The headline: **an intent is a record with limits that each name an owner and freedom you can test; once someone has said it back, a change inside that freedom is told, not asked, and records how it still serves the intent.** What reaches a person is ordered by a fixed rule from the intents it touches, never by a model; drift, of the work or of the world, is a question asked once.

**Answers to the prompt's questions:**
- *What Mandate's intent record needs to hold, and how to fill it without a form people hate.* Purpose (why); done looks like (each line bound to a fact, or marked "no measure"); key tasks (what must happen, whoever does it; never the method); limits (what must not happen, each with the one person who can lift it, an `inherits` list from the intent above, `tightens` where one narrows another); freedom (testable clauses: what, how far, until when); branches (an anticipated change, its order and cap); assumptions (watched, with a fact and a threshold); serves (one intent and one key task above); per-holder said-back state; review date. Not a form: it opens holding the intent above (limits locked, purpose seeded, measures picked from Data Atlas), the owner types only the difference, rules sort free text into fields, a premortem writes the limits, catchball keeps who moved what, and a rehearsal on last year's log finds gaps before it is passed down. A model may sort leftover sentences or draft a missing line, in pencil.
- *How "ranked by intent" works without a model deciding what matters, and where a model may help.* Items carry bindings (tags matched to intent records: an asset named in a key task, a warning named in a limit, a fact on an end state). A fixed rule, the same for everyone and owned by Mandate, orders them: 1 a limit and your own part, 2 your own part, 3 a limit above you, 4 a task or freedom above you, 5 a purpose or end state above you, 6 an intent you can't open (shown, with its holder), 7 none (folded, never hidden); ties go to what bites first. Each item says which intent and why it matters to you; a trace shows tag, record, part and rule. You correct a binding, never the order. A model may only suggest a binding for an untagged item, in pencil, until a person confirms it; it may also say the situation another way (a two-line summary) in pencil. It never ranks.
- *What "within intent" may unlock, and what identity and access would need.* For a holder who has said the intent back: approvals inside its freedom become notices (bringing a standby source into supply, moving supply between zones within licence, spend up to the floor's limit). Still asking: changing a branch's order, going past a branch's cap, anything outside freedom (the named holder of that choice), anything across a limit (its named owner, always). Identity needs a **derived grant**: a key that follows the intent, not issued by an admin, holding the freedom clause, the chain it came through, the backbrief that unlocked it, and an end (the review date, a change to that clause, or the person leaving the team), with an audit event "within intent" on the intent's Ref. Keycloak roles can't express this; it belongs in Mandate as policy evaluated server-side, checked against floors (ADR 0007). Agents never get it: the apprentice's ceiling stands, and an agent may only propose a change in pencil.

**Where these push back:**
- **Not everything should be ranked by intent.** Safety and security notices and anything restricted bypass the rule or keep their own urgency; an inherited limit reaches you at any reach setting; untouched items are folded, never dropped. Intent-ranking is an order, not a filter that hides.
- **Freedom waits for the backbrief.** The prompt says a person may change the plan without asking if it serves the intent. The run says: only once they have said it back. A new arrival at 03:00 still asks. That costs speed on rotation, and the cost is visible (open rings).
- **A written backbrief can become a ritual.** Bungay's backbrief is a conversation. The record should capture one, not replace it, and the owner's confirm or correct is the check.
- **Personal intents are overhead.** A part per person only where someone holds a distinct piece (Ellis's night feed); otherwise the team intent is enough.
- **"Everything below you in full" may be too much.** At Department scale, personal parts should show by title to anyone but their holder and lead.
- **No military words in what people read.** Plain words first ("said back", "limits", "freedom", "done looks like"); the doctrine words sit one step down behind T, and labels come from config so a deployment can use its own.
- **The Letterbox stays out of intent.** None of this is urgent by nature: a crossed limit is the limit owner's urgency (Water Quality's alert), not Mandate's. Drift and revisits are asked once, quietly.
- **No veto timers here either.** A branch's cap is a signal ahead, not a timer that acts; past it, someone decides.
