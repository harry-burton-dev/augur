# Surface 6 · The backbrief and drift

**The moment:** two kinds of checking. Understanding: on 27 Oct Ellis said TM-31 back in his own words; Sam confirmed with a correction (Kelda closes on Sundays). Of the nine people holding TM-31, six have said it back, one was corrected (Ines: "40 by Christmas" is a count, not the water), Tariq Noor joined yesterday on rotation and his backbrief arrives at 07:44, and Mo Clarke is on leave. Drift: since the local paper's story on 24 Oct, the repair gangs' hours on the 40 largest leaks fell from 72% to 44%; and this morning one of DEP-14's assumptions (both boreholes available) stopped holding, while another (Thornley stays under 60% before 1 Jan) is projected to cross about 23 Dec if the rain keeps on.

**Data:** `DATA.backbriefs` (who, state, their words, the parts their words cover and miss, corrections), `DATA.gaps`, `DATA.workDrift`, `DATA.track`, `DATA.xte`, the intents' `assumptions`. R replays, T shows doctrine words; G on V1.

**Rules all six share:** an intent passed down is an open question to each holder until they say it back and the owner confirms or corrects it; freedom within an intent unlocks only for confirmed holders. Drift is worked out from records, shown in a muted warm tone as a question for the owner, asked once at the crossing, never in the Letterbox and never as an alarm.

## V1: Say it back
*Idea.* Ellis's backbrief in his own words, each phrase matched to the part of TM-31 it covers (purpose, done looks like, a key task, L3, L4), and one phrase that is a limit of his own the intent doesn't have ("I won't take Thornley water… without telling Sam"). The intent on the left ticks what was said, greys what is someone else's part, and marks what wasn't said: the team's freedom, and five inherited limits. Sam confirms with his correction. Then today: "06:45: D1 (no planned interruption during an amber) is in the morning's first rank. It was one of the limits not said back on 27 Oct."

*Sources.* Stephen Bungay, *The Art of Action* (2011): the backbrief; ADP 6-0's confirmation brief; the reference desk's pencil and ink.

*What works.* "Understanding is confirmed, not assumed" made concrete: the match shows both what was understood and what wasn't, and the owner sees both before confirming. The phrase-to-part match can be pencil (a model) with the holder ticking each, or by hand with the gateway off. The self-imposed limit is the most interesting finding: people add their own caution, and it should be visible (Sam may want to say "you don't need to tell me; the branch allows it").

*What fails or risks.* A backbrief in writing loses the conversation that makes Bungay's version work; it should be the record of a conversation, not a substitute. People will learn to recite every limit. Matching phrases to parts is the weakest technical piece.

*Principles.* Question and answer: strong. Pencil and ink. Explain: the gaps are shown to the owner, not hidden as "confirmed".

*Needs.* Backbrief as a reply on the intent (ADR 0028's thread), with phrase-to-part links and a confirm/correct action for the owner.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V2: Three gaps
*Idea.* Bungay's three gaps as North Zone's working list for the fortnight. Knowledge (what we know against what we'd like to know: wrong fix, gather more information; right fix, say what matters), alignment (plans against actions: wrong fix, more detailed instructions; right fix, pass the intent down and have it said back), effects (expected against actual: wrong fix, more controls; right fix, freedom to adjust and a watch on drift). Each has this fortnight's cases, open or closed; at 1.2 s, Ellis's change closes an effects gap.

*Sources.* Bungay (2011); Clausewitz's friction, as Bungay reads it.

*What works.* It is the argument for the whole run in one page: every AUGUR piece answers one gap (the ranked situation, the backbrief, freedom and drift). Good for teaching team leads why the backbrief matters, and for a retrospective.

*What fails or risks.* Sorting cases into gaps is a judgement and slightly academic. As a daily page it duplicates the board and the drift views.

*Principles.* Calm. Plain words (the gap names are Bungay's and stay).

*Needs.* Nothing new; it reads from backbriefs, drift and change records.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 3

## V3: The said-back board
*Idea.* Sam's board: everyone who holds TM-31, with a ring (said back, corrected, open), their words, Sam's corrections, and what they didn't say. Tariq Noor's backbrief arrives at 07:44, the day after he joined from South Zone: it covers his task, his freedom and L3 but not the purpose, and waits with "Confirm" and "Correct: the purpose". Mo Clarke, on leave, is an open ring with "asked on return; works to the intent meanwhile, asking for any change".

*Sources.* Bungay; the paper-and-the-desk run's board; staff rotation practice.

*What works.* Rotation becomes visible and safe: a new arrival is an open ring, and until they say it back their changes still ask, so a rotating organisation doesn't silently hand freedom to someone who hasn't read the intent. The words people use are the best evidence of understanding a lead will ever get.

*What fails or risks.* A wall of quotations is a lot to read for a team of 30. Showing people's backbriefs to the whole team may embarrass; it should be the lead's view, and each person's own.

*Principles.* Question and answer: the open ring. Explain: why someone's changes still ask.

*Needs.* Holders per intent (from team membership), per-holder backbrief state, a join date for rotation.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V4: The work drifts
*Idea.* Where the repair gangs' hours went, four weeks: on the 40 largest leaks (72, 66, 51, 44%), on customer-reported small leaks (no intent), other. At this rate, 31 of 40 by 15 Dec, worked out from the repair log. Sam is asked once: keep the intent and steer the gangs back, amend TM-31 so small leaks are part of it, or ask Dana because it touches DEP-14 K2. Answered, it is not asked again unless the share drops another 10 points.

*Sources.* Cross-track error; the catalogue run's change arrives as work; the minute's revisit lines.

*What works.* "Nobody did anything wrong: the work followed the calls" is the right tone: drift is usually the situation pulling work, not people failing. The three answers include amending the intent, which is often the honest one (the public is calling about small leaks; maybe that matters now). It answers "work stops serving an intent" from records people already keep.

*What fails or risks.* Needs work tagged by intent or task (here, repair jobs carry a leak size); much knowledge work has no such log. A threshold for asking is a policy choice someone owns.

*Principles.* Calm: asked once, muted tone. Explain: worked out, with the source.

*Needs.* Work items tagged to intent tasks; a drift rule per task (share or rate) with an owner.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V5: The world drifts
*Idea.* Is "Rest Thornley this winter" still the right intent? Thornley's storage since 2 Oct against October's line to 75%, the projection at this week's rate, and DEP-14's revisit line (60% before 1 Jan, above which resting Thornley costs more energy than it saves). Beside it the assumptions both intents rest on: one crossed this morning (both boreholes available: asked once at 06:10), one watched and projected to cross about 23 Dec (Dana will be asked then, not before), and the Org's rainfall assumption holding (182% of average).

*Sources.* The minute's revisit lines; assumption-based planning (Dewar, RAND); Klein's premortem in reverse.

*What works.* It separates the two drifts the prompt asks for: here the people are fine and the intent may be wrong. "A question for Dana then, not a worry now" is calm made explicit. Writing assumptions into the intent at catchball (writing V3) is what makes this possible.

*What fails or risks.* Projections are the least reliable numbers in the run; the line must say how it was worked out and from what. Few intents will have measurable assumptions at first.

*Principles.* Calm: strong. Explain: every assumption shows its current reading.

*Needs.* Assumptions on intents as watched records with a fact and a threshold; a projection rule kept simple and stated.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V6: Cross-track error
*Idea.* Drift as a navigator reads it. Each intent on Ellis's chain is a course with a needle on a dotted scale: how far today's measure sits off the line, on which side, and whether the gap is closing. Kelda just behind and closing; North Zone's leaks behind and opening (the only needle in the drift tone); DEP-14's draw well ahead; the Org behind October's line and closing.

*Sources.* Cross-track error and the course deviation indicator in marine and air navigation.

*What works.* "Behind and closing needs nothing; off and opening is the question" is a better rule than any red/amber/green: it says what to do. One row per intent on the chain, so a person sees their whole nest's course in four lines.

*What fails or risks.* The dot scale (how much one dot is worth) is a per-measure judgement and can mislead; the readout under each needle carries the real numbers. Navigation instruments are unfamiliar to most office workers; the plain readout matters more than the needle.

*Principles.* Calm: no colour unless a gap opens. Same digits at every height: the readout's numbers are the facts'.

*Needs.* A "line today" for each end-state fact (straight line from start to target, or a stated curve); a scale per fact.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## Ranking
1. **V1 Say it back**: understanding confirmed, with what wasn't said shown to the owner.
2. **V3 The said-back board**: rotation as open rings, and freedom that waits for them.
3. **V4 The work drifts**: worked out from records, asked once, with "amend the intent" as an answer.
4. **V5 The world drifts**: the intent may be wrong while the people are right.
5. **V6 Cross-track error**: "off and opening" as the only thing that colours.
6. **V2 Three gaps**: the argument for the run; a teaching page.

**What I'd build first.** The backbrief as a reply on the intent with confirm/correct (V1), per-holder state shown as rings wherever the intent appears (V3), and drift as two watched kinds on the intent page (V4 for work, V5 for assumptions), each asking its owner once. V6's needle is the compact form for a person's own page.
