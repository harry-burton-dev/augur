# Surface 3 · The nest

**The moment:** the same morning, seen as structure. ORG-07 at the top; four Department intents under it (Water Resources, Customer, Water Quality and Network Operations' DEP-14) and one restricted one held by Security; three Team intents under DEP-14 (Trunk Mains, North Zone's TM-31, South Zone, which hasn't said its intent back); five personal parts under TM-31, Ellis Marsh's among them, and Tariq Noor's not yet said back because he joined yesterday on rotation.

**Data:** `DATA.intents` with `serves`, `servesPart` and `alsoServes`, `DATA.chain`, `DATA.xmatrix`, `DATA.orphans`, `DATA.spendCascade`. R replays, T shows doctrine words. Viewers differ per page and are named on it.

**The rule all six share:** two levels up in full; everything you hold below, in full; anything else by title, holder and state; a restricted intent by its holder only. Nothing on the plan is missing from any seat.

## V1: Floors as bands
*Idea.* The four floors stacked as bands in their floor colours. Ellis's chain runs through them as the lit path; floor travel (↑↓) racks focus from band to band, the far bands desaturated and softened, never hidden. The Org band is folded to one line because it is three up. Each band shows what sits beside the chain intent on that floor.

*Sources.* The cinematic run's focal planes and lit navigation path; floor travel; ADP 6-0 "two levels up".

*What works.* It is the shell's own language (floors, travel, focus) applied to intent, so it needs no new mental model. Siblings beside each band answer "who else serves the same thing?" at a glance. Folding the Org rather than hiding it makes "two levels up" a reading distance, not a permission.

*What fails or risks.* Four bands at full height leave little room for content; at 1280 purposes clamp to two lines. Rack focus on text is a cost: the far bands must stay readable (blur kept to a token fraction). A person on two teams breaks the single chain.

*Principles.* Focus stacking: strong. Calm: one rack at a time. Explain: folded says "open it any time".

*Needs.* `serves` per intent; the far-plane blur as a token (`--x-proposed-far-blur`, a fraction of `--space-1`).

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5

## V2: The lit path
*Idea.* The whole nest as a tree: Org, five Department intents, three Teams under DEP-14, five parts under TM-31. Ellis's chain to the Org is drawn lit and the rest is the far plane, readable and quiet. Selecting a node shows its purpose and where it sits relative to you.

*Sources.* The cinematic run's lit path; Hoshin Kanri's cascade.

*What works.* The clearest single picture of the nest and of "how my part serves both above". The restricted Security intent sits in the tree as a hatch, which is the honest version of compartmented work: you know it exists and serves the same Org intent. Good as the Explorer view of Mandate.

*What fails or risks.* Trees don't scale: a real Org intent may have 40 Department and 300 Team intents beneath it. It needs folding by default below one level off the path. Selection detail repeats V1.

*Principles.* Focus stacking; explain, never hide.

*Needs.* A tree layout in the kit (or Explorer's); the lit path from the cinematic kit.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V3: The X-matrix, unfolded
*Idea.* Hoshin's X-matrix reduced to its two working quadrants: every key task on every floor against the measures it moves (strong or some), and who holds it. Gaps show both ways: ORG-07 K3, "keep customers told, in one voice", moves no measure; "interruptions over 12 hours" has no task that moves it directly.

*Sources.* Hoshin Kanri's X-matrix (Akao); the catalogue run's the structure predicts its gaps.

*What works.* It is the one view that checks the nest is sound rather than just showing it. "A task with no measure can only be argued about" is a sharp finding for an Org head. It uses facts already bound to end states.

*What fails or risks.* Dense and unfamiliar; most people would never open it. The strong/some marks are judgements someone has to make and keep current. The full X-matrix's other quadrants (objectives against each other) are left out on purpose.

*Principles.* Explain: the gaps are named, not coloured. Calm.

*Needs.* A task-to-measure link with a strength; facts on end states.

*Scores.* Clarity 3 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 3

## V4: Coverage
*Idea.* The Org head's view: every key task and limit of ORG-07, which intents serve it, and how many people below have said it back (a small bar of filled and hollow cells). A limit held by its owner directly says so ("no intent needed"). The restricted intent shows as "part not shown to you, ask Nadia Kerr", even to the Chief Executive. A side column lists work that serves no intent: 14 customer-reported small leaks taking 48% of North Zone's gang hours, and a meter programme planned before ORG-07.

*Sources.* Hoshin cascade and OKR alignment (orphan objectives); the label run's need-to-know.

*What works.* "Work that serves no intent" is the most useful thing on the page and the bridge to drift: it is how a Department or Org head finds the gap between the plan and the week. The said-back bar makes understanding countable without a survey. Showing the restricted line to the CEO is right: compartments are about content, not existence.

*What fails or risks.* Finding orphan work needs every job and programme to carry an intent tag, which most systems won't have at first. "Serves no intent" may read as an accusation; the copy says "not wrong by default".

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: a list, asked once.

*Needs.* An intent tag on jobs, programmes and documents (an Artifacts and Data Atlas field), and a count of untagged work by floor.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4

## V5: Limits flow down
*Idea.* The nest read by its limits: rows are limits, columns are the four intents on Ellis's chain. Each cell says set here, inherited (double bar) or tightened by (L5's £250k becomes D2's £50k). Under it, freedom to spend on one change narrows floor by floor: £250k, £50k, £25k, £25k, each citing where it comes from.

*Sources.* Schemes of delegation and signing limits in finance; the label run's strictest wins.

*What works.* It answers "what may I do without asking?" with its provenance, which is what an auditor and a new starter both want. The narrowing bar makes "never wider than the floor above" visible, and the rule is enforceable on save. It is the bridge to keys and doors (N): these are grants derived from intent.

*What fails or risks.* Only quantitative limits narrow cleanly; most limits are yes/no. A table of "inherited" cells is mostly repetition. Someone might read inherited as "not important here".

*Principles.* Explain: every number cites its source. Calm.

*Needs.* `tightens` on limits; spend freedom as a number with a unit; the identity rules to turn freedom into a time-boxed grant.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 3 · Feasibility 5 · Fit 4

## V6: From any floor
*Idea.* The same nest from four seats (1–4: Priya, Dana, Sam, Ellis). One rule decides each card: yours; two above in full; everything that serves yours, all the way down, in full; anything else by title, holder and state; a restricted one by its holder only. The side counts what each seat sees in each way.

*Sources.* ADP 6-0 "two levels up"; need-to-know compartments; the label run's four kinds of no.

*What works.* It makes the visibility rule checkable: anyone can see what someone else sees, which is the start of trusting it. The counts show the rule is generous downward (Dana sees all ten intents below her in full) and modest upward, which is mission command's shape. Nothing disappears between seats.

*What fails or risks.* "Full" for everything below may be too much for a Department with hundreds of personal parts; personal parts may need to be by title for anyone but the owner and their lead. Showing other people's seats is itself a disclosure decision.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Many deployments: the rule is fixed; the nest is data.

*Needs.* The visibility rule as a Mandate policy evaluated server-side (ADR 0007 floors plus intent ancestry); a restricted flag with a holder.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## Ranking
1. **V1 Floors as bands**: the nest in the shell's own grammar, with two up as a reading distance.
2. **V6 From any floor**: the visibility rule made checkable.
3. **V4 Coverage**: the Org head's view, and work that serves no intent.
4. **V5 Limits flow down**: what you may do without asking, with its provenance.
5. **V2 The lit path**: the best picture, the worst at scale.
6. **V3 The X-matrix, unfolded**: an analyst's check, not a daily view.

**What I'd build first.** The visibility rule from V6 as Mandate policy, V1's bands as the nest view on a person's Mandate page (folding further up), and V4's coverage with orphan work for Department and Org heads. V5's narrowing becomes the "what I may decide" panel on any intent.
