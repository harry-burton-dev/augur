## V1: From the Org to Ellis, and back (`v1-org-to-ellis.html`)
The required flow: an Org intent followed down to a person, through a surprise that breaks the plan, a change made within intent without asking, and the floor above seeing why. Six steps (1–6 or ← →), with the nest on the left racking focus to the floor each step happens on.

*What it shows.*
1. **2 Oct · Org.** Priya Raman decides ORG-07: purpose, done looks like as three measured lines, five limits each with the one person who can lift it, freedom, no method.
2. **9 Oct · Department.** DEP-14 serves ORG-07 K1. Catchball moved the end state from 50 Ml/d by 31 Dec to 60 by 31 Jan (from Lewis Baird, who runs the link); a branch was added (Ryecroft first, then Thornley for up to 72 hours); two limits added; passed down to three teams, South Zone still an open ring.
3. **27 Oct · You.** Ellis says his part back in his own words; Sam corrects him (Kelda closes on Sundays). Saying it back unlocks a key derived from DEP-14's freedom, until 15 Dec. The chain in one breath.
4. **06:10 · the plan breaks.** Borehole 3 out on a quality alert. Ellis's morning ranked by the intents each item touches; Kelda's measure with a ghost at 2 of 10; L3 keeps borehole 3 out and names Hana Sato.
5. **07:40 · changed within intent.** Six options with verdicts by rule; Ellis takes branch B1 (Ryecroft +6, Thornley +2): 8 of 8, 6 Ml of Thornley (0.07 points), £18k of £25k, nobody needs to say yes; within DEP-14, 7 of 7 limits held, told Sam, Dana and Hana.
6. **07:52 · the floors above see why.** Dana gets a notice, "told, not asked", with the so-that chain from Ellis's task to her intent, "Seen" and "Ask a question" (which undoes nothing), and, separately, the crossed assumption asked once. Priya sees one folded line on ORG-07: one change within intent in Network Operations, its cost and that all Org limits held.

*From.* Grammar V1, V5, V6; writing V3 (catchball), V4 (branch from a story); nest V1 (bands); situation V1, V5; within V1, V2, V4, V6; backbrief V1.

*What works.* It shows mission command's economics: the expensive thinking happened in October (catchball, the branch, the backbrief), so the change at 07:40 costs one decision and one record, and the floor above learns why without having been asked. "Two levels up sees the change and why; three up sees it folded" is the nest rule doing real work. Every number on step 5 is in the intent's own units.

*What fails or risks.* Six steps is a story, not a screen; in the product each step is the page it came from. The step 4 list and step 5 options are shortened versions of their surfaces. A real Org intent would have hundreds of parts below; the bands show one chain.

*Principles.* Focus stacking: the nest racks to the floor of each step. Explain, never hide: every limit names its owner; every notice names its freedom. Calm: nothing flashes; the Letterbox is not used, because nothing here needs a decision before someone's next look. Question and answer: the backbrief answers the passed-down intent; the crossed assumption is asked once.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V2: Ellis's morning (`v2-ellis-morning.html`)
A person's Home on the morning the plan breaks. The chain two up as a band at the top (his part so that TM-31 so that DEP-14 so that ORG-07, the Org dimmed). What reached him, ranked by the rule, each with why; the rank-1 item opened on the right as a change: the Kelda measure with its ghost, six options with verdicts, what the chosen pair touches before it is recorded (Thornley 41.2% → 41.13%, Ryecroft 6 of 6 with no headroom, £18k of £25k, customers none), then the record. At 1.2 s he picks Ryecroft and the Thornley branch; the ghost moves back to 10 of 10; it records, and the list row now carries "changed within DEP-14 · 07:40".

*From.* Situation V1 and V5; within V1, V2, V3; nest V1 (the chain band).

*What works.* It is the answer to "given what we're trying to achieve, what does this mean for me, and what may I do without asking?" on one screen. The item and the change stay together: the row carries the stamp afterwards, so the morning reads as handled. Nobody's yes needed is said in words, not implied by a green button.

*What fails or risks.* Option lists will often be empty or partial; the panel must work with "write an option" and the branch as the only pre-written one. The panel is busy at 1280. Ranking ties within rank 2 depend on bite times that many items won't have.

*Principles.* Calm: nothing moves until a person acts. Explain: verdicts and the rule. Pencil: no model in this screen; with the gateway on, a model could draft options in pencil from the branch plan.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5

## V3: Writing DEP-14 (`v3-writing-dep14.html`)
The intent editor in Mandate. It opens holding the intent above: serves chosen first, purpose seeded, done-looks-like as facts with live measures, limits with five locked and each added one carrying its premortem story, catchball seams (round, old words, who moved it) on the end state, a task and the branch, and the assumptions that will be watched. On the right, the rule checks, the rehearsal on last winter (which finds one gap at 1.2 s and offers a freedom line in pencil), and passing down, which makes each team an open ring.

*From.* Writing V5 (fill from above), V4 (premortem stories), V3 (catchball seams), V6 (rehearse), V1 (rules first, pencil for leftovers); grammar V4 (passed down is an open question).

*What works.* The form isn't hated because it isn't empty, and every line says where it came from: given, typed, a story, a seam. Rehearsing before passing down is the cheapest place to find a gap. "Each team gets it as an open question: freedom unlocks when they say it back" ties writing to the backbrief.

*What fails or risks.* Measures show today's values on a draft written in October (the prototype reuses the live facts); a real draft would show values at the time of writing. Long. People who don't run a premortem or catchball get a plainer form, which is fine.

*Principles.* Explain: provenance on every line. Pencil and ink. Strictest wins on save.

*Scores.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 4

## V4: The intent's page, as its owner reads it (`v4-dana-floor.html`)
DEP-14's page for Dana. Header: worth revisiting, asked once at 06:10. Left: the teams below with rings (North Zone's eight holders as small rings, 6 of 8 said back; South Zone not said back, so its changes still ask) and each team's cross-track reading. Middle: changes made within DEP-14 this week, each told not asked, each with "so that" and its cost; Ellis's arrives at 1.2 s; under them, what still asks (Rita Gomez's request, because South Zone hasn't said DEP-14 back). Right: drift of the world (the crossed assumption, with keep or amend) and drift of the work (North Zone's gang hours, asked of Sam, shown here because it touches DEP-14 K2).

*From.* Within V6 (notices and still asks); backbrief V3 (rings), V4 (work drift), V5 (world drift), V6 (cross-track); grammar V4 (states).

*What works.* It is the floor above's whole relationship with an intent on one page: who understood it, what they did within it and why, what they still need from you, and whether it still fits the world. Nothing on it needs pressing except the two real questions. It makes the case that "approvals become notices" leaves a manager better informed, not less.

*What fails or risks.* At Department scale (30 teams, hundreds of parts) the left column needs grouping and the middle a filter by cost. Showing Sam's drift on Dana's page could read as going over Sam's head; the copy says it is asked of him.

*Principles.* Calm: strong, and said on the page. Question and answer: notices vs asks vs asked-once are visibly different kinds. Explain.

*Scores.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5
