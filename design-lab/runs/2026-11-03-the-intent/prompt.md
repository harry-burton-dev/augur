# Prompt AC: The intent. Applying intent to context.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-intent/prompt.md`.

---

Batch 6 is about **thinking made visible**. This prompt is the last step
of the climb: once people have context, **intent** says what to do with
it.

Mandate holds intents: what a floor means to achieve and why, and the
parts passed down to the teams and people who'll do it. An intent written
once and filed is a slogan. It becomes useful when it meets a situation:
"given what we are trying to achieve, what does this new information
mean for me, and what may I do about it without asking?" Armies learned
this the hard way and call it mission command: say the purpose and the
end state, leave the method to the people on the ground, and they can
act when the plan breaks. This run asks how AUGUR could **apply intent to
context**: filter and rank what a person sees by the intents they serve,
show how their work nests under the intents above them, and let them
act within intent with the reason visible. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-intent/`.

## Direction
- **Purpose, end state, limits.** An intent says why, what done looks
  like, and what must not happen; the method is left open on purpose.
- **Two levels up.** Everyone can see the intent of their own floor and
  the floor above it, and how their part serves both.
- **Intent filters context.** What reaches a person is ranked by the
  intents it touches, and says which one.
- **Act within intent.** A person may change the plan without asking if
  the change still serves the intent; the change records how.
- **Understanding is confirmed, not assumed**: the person given an
  intent says it back in their own words (the backbrief) before work
  starts.
- **Drift is visible**: when work stops serving an intent, or an intent
  stops matching the situation, it shows.
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Mission command** (Moltke's *Auftragstaktik*; US ADP 6-0, 2019; UK
  doctrine). Take: commander's intent as purpose, key tasks and end
  state; disciplined initiative; "two levels up".
- **Stephen Bungay, *The Art of Action* (2011)**: the knowledge, alignment
  and effects gaps; the backbrief. Take: briefing and backbriefing as
  the way intent is passed down and checked.
- **Gary Klein's research on intent statements** (*Sources of Power*,
  1998). Take: the pieces a good intent carries (purpose, objective,
  sequence, rationale, key decisions, anti-goals, constraints).
- **Hoshin Kanri and "catchball"**, and **OKRs** (Grove; Doerr). Take:
  intent negotiated up and down the floors, not only handed down.
- **Bratman's belief-desire-intention model** of practical reasoning.
  Take: an intention as a commitment that filters which options are even
  considered.
- **The five-paragraph order** (situation, mission, execution, support,
  command) as a reminder that situation comes before mission.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Intent sheet** (grammar): purpose, end state, key tasks, limits,
   freedom, nested under, backbrief, drift.
2. **Writing an intent**: a Department head writes one in Mandate with
   help to separate purpose from method.
3. **The nest**: Org, Department, Team and You intents as one view, with
   two levels up visible from any floor.
4. **Intent meets the situation**: a person's incoming context ranked by
   the intents it touches, with a line on why each matters to them.
5. **Acting within intent**: a plan breaks; the person changes it, sees
   whether the change stays inside the limits, and records how it serves
   the intent.
6. **The backbrief and drift**: confirming understanding, and spotting
   work or intents that have drifted apart.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must follow an Org intent down to
a person, through a surprise that breaks the plan, a change made within
intent without asking, and the floor above seeing why.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- What Mandate's intent record needs to hold (purpose, end state,
  limits, freedom) and how to fill it without a form people hate.
- How "ranked by intent" works without a model deciding what matters,
  and where a model may help, in pencil.
- What "within intent" may unlock (for example approvals that become
  notices) and what the identity and access rules would need (see keys
  and doors, N).
