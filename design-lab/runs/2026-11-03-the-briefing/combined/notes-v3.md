Reach counts come from the `groups` map (68 records kept on three floors, one restricted) and the floor rule in docs/atlas.md ("a floor shows what's kept on it and on the floors above"), plus one detail the round-1 data lacked: a record kept on a Team floor opens only to that team, so a Westfield reader is told "some locked".

**Keys.** 1, 2 and 3 open each choice, ↑↓ preview the options (the sentence rewrites as you move), ↵ chooses, and with no list open ↵ sends. I inks the bottom line, W jumps a week, P republishes after the seam, T shows admin words, G turns the model gateway off, R replays. The moment plays about 1.2 s after load: the library list opens and previews North depot notes, Board papers and Fleet briefings in turn, then closes on Fleet briefings. The proofs appear and Send is blocked by the pencil line. Checked at 1600×900 and 1280×800, in the sent and week-on states, with T and G, and with reduced motion. No console errors.

## V3: Finding to action
*Idea.* The finished briefing goes out as an order in three sentences, and only the underlined words are choices: "Publish it to ⟨Fleet briefings⟩. 34 people on the Department floor and under it can read it. Readers outside North depot crew can open 29 of 68 records behind it; their copy says to ask Jo Barnes or Health and safety desk for the rest. Feed it to ⟨M-42 · Keep every fleet vehicle roadworthy⟩… Watch it ⟨for any change⟩. Only you are told. AUGUR can notice 3 of 7 things that would move it." Each option in a list shows its reach before you pick it: people, the least any reader can open, and whether it needs approval. Under the order are the **proofs**, one per reader the choice reaches (a reader on Sam's own team, a reader elsewhere on the Department floor, and the intent's owner as she will see it in Mandate), side by side. Each part of the briefing shows a link, "some locked" or a locked hatch, and each copy says whom to ask. While the bottom line is pencil, every proof shows a corner-ticked "won't go out until Sam inks it", and Send is blocked with the reason. On the right, the **watch** lists the seven things that would move the answer, each tagged with the reason it would give. The four AUGUR can't notice yet are drawn dashed with what they need. A due date passing is one of them, because it makes no event. **W** jumps a week. On 5 Nov two due dates pass and nobody is told. On 7 Nov Jo records NV-03's inspection, which is an event, so the watch re-runs. The notice names one of the five reasons ("Something happened") and explains the arithmetic: one fewer, plus the two whose dates passed on 5 Nov, which you hear about two days late. The published briefing shows a **seam**: the old 7 struck, a dashed blue edge, "8 of 18 now", the date and reason, and the line extended across a seam mark. Readers see both until Sam republishes.

*Sources.* The order in a sentence (action V4); proofs (action V3); flood marks' list of what moves the count and what AUGUR can't notice (action V5); five reasons an answer moves and "share the question, not the answer" (reference desk shortlist); six ways to be empty and "some, never a number" for readers (catalogue shortlist); pencil and ink; the catalogue's change seam (a notice that folds to a seam).

*What works.*
- Reach is read before acting, at three grains in one view: a number in the sentence, a meter per option, and each reader's actual copy. Moving the list to Board papers turns "29 of 68" into "1 of 68" and adds "It waits for Priya Nair's approval" as you move through the list, before anything is chosen.
- The proofs show what the sentence can only claim. Ana's and Priya's copies have five of six parts edge-marked "some locked". Jo's copy is all links, but still says the incident report is with the Health and safety desk. Readers see "some", and the publisher sees exact counts ("you see: opens 29 of 68").
- The pencil block is enforced in two places that agree: the proofs (the corner-ticked gap where the bottom line would be) and the Send bar, which gives the reason and both ways out (ink it, or G for the template line).
- The watch is honest about the events service. Three triggers are lit and four are dashed with their needs. The week jump makes the biggest gap felt: the count went up on the 5th, and you only hear about it because something else happened on the 7th. If nothing had happened, the watch would have stayed silent while the answer was wrong.
- The seam keeps what went out and what is true now side by side, with the reason. Nothing is silently rewritten in a published document.

*What fails or risks.*
- Density. Three columns of mostly small type at 1280×800: the left briefing and the watch scroll. The notice scrolls into view on W, but only one of the seam and the notice is in view at a time on a short screen.
- Near-identical proofs. Ana's and Priya's copies are the same here because both open 29 of 68. The difference is where they read it (a library or an intent's evidence), and the eye has to find that. A "differs from yours" summary line per proof would help.
- "Readers outside North depot crew" assumes the proof readers are representative. A real reach query must find the least-access reader on each floor, not a sample person.
- Whom to ask can be long ("Jo Barnes, Priya Nair or Health and safety desk" on Board papers). It needs a cap and "and 2 more".
- The seam is one moved number. A briefing with several moved numbers needs a seam per sentence and a summary at the top, or it turns into a diff.
- The watch fired for "any change". A threshold preset ("more than 8") would have stayed silent at 8. That is right, but the list should show the preset's effect ("this would not have told you").

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: the strongest of the b-screens. Locked parts stay in every copy, the unnoticeable triggers are listed with their needs, and the seam keeps the old number. Calm: the demo plays once, the week jump is on request, and nothing loops. The only colour at rest is the floor and reach ink. Focus stacking: the previewed sentence is lit (left edge) while the list is open. Letterbox: not used. A watch that crosses a threshold close to an intent's deadline would be the urgent sentence (PDR-009), drawn by the shell, not by this page.

*Model gateway.* The model writes nothing on this screen except the bottom line it drafted in V2, still pencil. The order sentences, reach, proofs, triggers, notice and seam are all templates over queries, because they are commitments or facts. With G off, the template bottom line goes out in ink and every number stays the same. The notice must never be model-written: its reason is picked from the five by the events service, not inferred.

*Data Atlas, Artifacts, Mandate and the events service would need.*
1. **A reach query** per target (library, intent): people per floor, the least any reader opens, and whom to ask per group. It must honour the floor rule, team-kept records and restricted records, and be computed on the server ("proof as reader X"), not faked on the client.
2. **The watch stores the recipe and the watcher's access**, never the count, and re-runs on events. It needs a **cursor** so it resumes where it left off, the way Data Atlas already follows Mandate.
3. **Events with a reason**: `atlas.observation.recorded` (happened, or "we learned about the past" when `observedAt` is in the past), `artifacts.document.withdrawn` (Data Atlas already follows it), and new: `atlas.triage.resolved` (a disagreement was settled), `atlas.ontology.changed` (a word changed meaning), `identity.membership.changed` (what you can see changed).
4. **A clock in the events service**: nightly re-runs and "date reached" events. Without it, the 5 Nov change in this screen is heard only by luck.
5. **A re-run diff** that explains the change by record (−NV-03, +NV-11 +NV-15), so the notice can say which part was an event and which part was time.
6. **A seam in Artifacts**: a published sentence keeps its as-sent value and shows the moved value beside it with date and reason, until the author republishes.
7. Mandate: an "evidence" view for an intent, so the owner's proof is real.

*Needs (tokens).* New: `--x-proposed-seam` (the dashed edge and labels where a published number moved) and `--x-proposed-seam-wash`, both mixes of `--info` and `--ink`. Carried: the pencil, lit, disagree, unseen, gap, quiet and type-micro tokens (see notes-v2). Relies on `--font-mono` (needs an ADR). Kit parts: the slot-in-a-sentence with a reach listbox, the proof card, the trigger row (can / can't), the seam.

*Score.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 4.

*Beyond the ask.* (1) Team-kept records open only to that team. The round-1 data treated a Team floor as one place, which overstated what a Westfield reader could open. (2) The week jump shows a change that is a mix of an event and time passing, and the notice separates the two. A watch that names only one reason would have been wrong. (3) Republishing (P) is offered, never automatic: a seam is the honest default.

*Questions for Harry.* Should a published briefing's numbers ever update in place, or always show a seam until the author republishes? I recommend always a seam. And does the clock in the events service go on the roadmap now? Every watch on an overdue-style finding depends on it.
