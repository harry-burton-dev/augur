# Round 2 · combined screens

Four screens that could ship, built from the round-1 winners. All use the same deployment and the same renewal. **R** replays, **T** shows trade names, **G** turns the model off (no mark, count, word or result changes on any of the four).

## V1: The renewal, end to end
*Idea.* The flow the prompt asked for. Facilities decides whether to renew Calder Waste, in five stages along the top (← → or 1 to 5): **how renewals like this go** (11 past cases as dots, 4 of 11 recovered within 6 months; no waste tender here took under 4 months against 3 available, so "five years" and "end it now" are dropped), **why service fell** (the grid, road closures ruled out, too few vehicles for more homes), **what the plan rests on** (seven assumptions marked by rule, five below the bar, each with a hedge), **imagine it failed** (reasons sorted into changes, watches and one accepted risk), and **the decision with its working** (words, status, below its bar with a hedge, six watches, revisit rule, options set aside and kept). Two things persist across stages: **the draft at the top changes as each technique changes a line** (struck and inserted, numbered by stage), and **the working builds up on the right** (one result label per technique, minutes said and taken).

*From.* Grammar V4 (result label), choose V3 (pause points), explanations V1 and V3, assumptions V1 and V5, premortem V3, kept V1 and V4.

*What works.* The live draft is the best idea of round 2: you watch each technique earn its minutes by changing a line, and by stage 5 the redline *is* the decision's history. The order (outside view, explanations, assumptions, premortem) is the order a decision needs them, and nothing is offered out of turn. 81 minutes over three weeks for a contract decision is plausible.

*What fails or risks.* Five stages on one screen is an overview; each stage is a compact version of a round-1 surface and would open fully on click. The draft band grows to two lines by stage 4. The outside view stage is dense at 1280.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: very strong. Calm: one change lights per stage. Focus: the lit stage and fresh result. Letterbox: not used; a decision due inside a week with a load-bearing unsupported assumption *could* earn it, but I'd keep it out (see the shortlist's push-backs).

*Test.* The working claims each technique changed a line. On this decision it did (five numbered changes). Across a quarter, the share of runs with "changed: nothing" should stay below a quarter, or the order is wrong.

*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 5 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5.

## V2: A part any app opens
*Idea.* The bench as a **kit part**. In an Artifacts briefing, Sam selects "Calder Waste says most missed collections were caused by road closures"; a sheet opens beside the document (the document rack-focuses back, not hidden) and offers **one** technique by rule (it explains a fall, and it is the supplier's own explanation). When it is done, the result attaches to the sentence as a chip and a seam ("tested · road closures has most against it"). Six numbered pins show the part's contract: opened by any app on a selection; one technique by rule; reads facts and intents by `Ref` with the reader's access; runs beside the work; leaves a result record with its own `Ref`; says so on the events service.

*From.* Choose V4 (margin), grammar V4 and V6, explanations V1.

*What works.* It answers "where does the bench belong?" by showing it: a sheet any app can open, which never leaves the app and leaves a record any app can cite. The briefing's sentence now carries how it was tested at word size.

*What fails or risks.* A full 10 × 6 grid doesn't fit a sheet; the sheet needs the compact form (three facts that decide it) with "open on the bench" for the rest. Selections on prose need sentence-level citations from Artifacts.

*Principles.* Focal planes: the document steps back while the sheet is open. Explain, never hide. Calm.

*Test.* Any app should be able to open the sheet with only an output `Ref` and a selection; if an app needs to know about techniques, the boundary is wrong.

*Score.* Clarity 4 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5.

## V3: From working to watches
*Idea.* What the working does after the meeting. Left: the six watches D-142 left, each saying what it listens to or why it can't see. Middle: for a chosen event, **the chain that runs**: an event (depot log: 8 vehicles in the week of 18 Jan) → watch W1 crossed → assumption a1 failing (the weakest) → D-142 *worth revisiting* (the words don't change; the status beside them does) → the hedge written at the table is offered → the chair is asked once. ← → shows 25 Jan (the 9th vehicle: back in force, nobody asked) and 12 May (no event: a date passed; found by hand 12 days late). Right: six pins for **what the events service would need**, two marked "missing today".

*From.* Assumptions V4 and V5, premortem V6, the briefing run's watches, the minute run's revisit lines.

*What works.* It is the answer to the prompt's third question in one picture, and the 12 May chain is the argument for a clock. "The hedge is offered, written at the table" shows why hedges matter: nobody has to invent the response on the day.

*What fails or risks.* The chains are narrative; the real thing needs every link to be a record (event, watch, assumption, decision status, Inbox item). One Inbox item per crossing could still be too many on a busy floor.

*Principles.* Dark when normal (a watch going quiet asks nobody). Watches say what they can't see. Status beside the words.

*Test.* For every watch AUGUR can see, a crossing marks the decision worth revisiting within a day and asks exactly one person. For every watch it can't, the decision lists it before it is signed.

*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 3 · Fit 5.

## V4: A year later
*Idea.* Nadia Osei opens D-142 on 18 Nov 2027: the words and status, the seams from two "worth revisiting" episodes, **how it was tested and what happened** (said / happened, per technique), a score strip (6 of 7 assumptions held; the forecast happened; 3 watches crossed and seen; 1 missed for 12 days because AUGUR had no clock), and three questions she can ask the working, including the one it refuses to answer for her ("Should I renew it again?": it offers a technique instead, with this decision as one more case).

*From.* Kept V1, V3 and V6; the minute run's status beside the words.

*What works.* The working is kept *and* scored, and the score feeds the next decision's outside view. It reads in a minute.

*What fails or risks.* Outcomes need recording; some will come from watches, some from the owner at the review date. It depends on the decision record type the minute run proposed.

*Principles.* Explain, never hide: strong. Calm: still.

*Test.* A new head of a team, with no handover, should answer "why 12 months, and did it work?" from this page in under two minutes.

*Score.* Clarity 5 · Distinctiveness 4 · Feasibility 4 · Fit 5.
