# Shortlist: the analyst's bench

Ranked by what I'd build first. The headline: **offer one technique at a time, by rule, with its price in minutes said first; run it on the organisation's own facts; keep the result beside the decision it shaped, and score it when the future arrives.** The model widens (an explanation, a reason, a case against, a fact to find) and never narrows (it never marks, ranks, drops, chooses or sets a word). Turning it off changes no mark, count or result on any page.

Every claim below carries a **test**: what would show it wrong.

**Answers to the prompt's questions:**
- *Which six techniques ship first, and which are left to training?*
  - **Ship, in this order:** how things like this usually go (outside view, 10 min), competing explanations (ACH, 25 min), check the assumptions (15 min), imagine it failed (premortem, 20 min, 3 to 8 people), check the sources (quality of information check, 10 min), the case against (devil's advocacy, 15 min). The first four are the order a decision needs them, and the renewal shows each paying. The last two are cheap, and are offered when a sentence rests on one source or a person says they lean one way.
  - **Left to training**, named on the bench with why: Team A / Team B and red team (need prepared teams and days), high impact low probability and what if (overused without practice), scenarios (a workshop, not a task), Fermi estimates (no facts to run on), fishbone (competing explanations covers it; its empty bones survive as a premortem view), and five whys (see push-backs).
- *Does the bench belong in Explorer, Mandate, a kit part, or the shell?* **A kit part, with its records in Mandate and its home in Explorer; never the shell.**
  - **A Quill kit part** (the sheet and the result label) that any app opens on its own output with a `Ref` and a selection. Apps never import the bench; they ask the kit for the sheet. That is how a technique reaches the point of work in Artifacts, Mandate or Data Atlas.
  - **Result records in Mandate**, as a `working` record type with its own `Ref`, cited by the output it shaped. Almost every result shapes an intent or a decision, and the decision record (minute run) already lives there. A result on a briefing with no intent still gets a home there, under the briefing's floor.
  - **Explorer hosts the bench as a place**: the key, the shelf, the floor's record and score, and the full grid when a sheet is too small.
  - **Not the shell**: the shell owns focus and loudness, not methods. The sheet uses the shell's focal planes; nothing more.
- *How do results feed watches, how is confidence worked out, and what would the events service need?*
  - Each assumption's **sign** and each premortem reason sorted as "watch" becomes a **watch** on the decision: a saved question in Data Atlas, a line, an owner, and a list of what it can't see.
  - **Confidence**: marks are worked out from each fact's yardstick word by one rule; a person may only lower one. The decision is no surer than its weakest load-bearing assumption and names it. Below the decision kind's bar, it may still be decided **if every assumption below the bar has a hedge and a sign**. When a fact changes, the word is worked out again; a watch never changes a word itself.
  - A crossing marks the decision **worth revisiting** (the minute run's status beside the words), offers the hedge, and asks the chair once. A watch going quiet asks nobody.
  - **The events service would need**: fact-changed events with old and new values; **a clock** (dates passing as events) and **silence as an event** (both missing today: two of six watches here depend on them, and the tender line was missed for 12 days); lines tested on saved questions with the watcher's access; a reason on every notice (one of the five reasons an answer moves); a "can't see" list published with each watch; and `working.attached` events so decisions and the floor's score can pick results up.

**Where these push back:**
- **Five whys is left to training,** although the prompt lists it with Ishikawa. It follows one chain and stops at the first plausible cause, which is the trap competing explanations exists to avoid. Road closures would have survived five whys.
- **Cynefin is a sorter, not a technique.** Its real gift to the bench is the right to say **"no technique"** in a clear or chaotic situation. A bench that always offers a method will be ignored by the people who know better.
- **"Cheap to start, quick to finish" isn't true of a premortem** if you count people: five people for 20 minutes is 100 minutes of the floor. Every price says minutes *and* people, and striped bars show it.
- **The letterbox stays out.** A decision due within a week on an unsupported load-bearing assumption is important, not urgent; the hedge is the answer, not a frame.
- **The model is capped as well as penciled.** One proposed explanation per run and one premortem reason, or it floods the grid with plausible noise, and untested explanations can't enter a result.
- **The prompt's three marks need two more.** "Not known yet" and "not yours to see" can't be marked solid, caveated or unsupported; they follow the briefing run's rule and stay visible.
- **Scores are kept for floors and techniques, never for people.** A score that can blame will be gamed.
- **The premortem's imagined headline must never leave the room** (no share, no export): a fake news page about a real supplier is a leak risk.
- **"Run on real facts" depends on records most organisations don't keep**: contract outcomes with a reason, source authorship. Where they're missing, the technique says "no class yet" or "no reason recorded", and runs on what there is.

**Decisions for Harry** (none taken):
1. **Where the bench lives.** Recommended: a Quill kit part, results as a `working` record type in Mandate, the bench's home in Explorer, nothing in the shell. It needs an AUGUR ADR for the `working` `Ref` type and a Mandate record for the record type.
2. **Hedged below the bar.** May a decision go ahead below its kind's standard of proof if every assumption below the bar has a written hedge and a sign? Recommended yes; it extends the briefing run's standard of proof.
3. **Pause points per decision kind** (three, each with a default technique and a fallback), set by the kind's owner in Mandate? Recommended yes.
4. **Widen, never narrow**, enforced in the model gateway as a refusal list per technique? Recommended yes.
5. **The six techniques fixed for every deployment?** Recommended fixed and translated; a deployment may switch one off (it stays on the bench, greyed, with who switched it off and why), not add its own.
6. **Keep the score**, outcomes recorded at the review date, visible to the floor, never per person? Recommended yes.
7. **The events service's clock and silence events**: now the briefing, minute and bench runs all depend on them. Put them on the roadmap?
8. **Group sessions** (premortem cards hidden until everyone has written) need shared live state. Is that in scope for the kit, or a Mandate feature?

**Gaps:**
- *Tokens.* New: `--x-proposed-against` (a mark against an explanation), `--x-proposed-cell` (one grid cell), `--x-proposed-hedge` (a fallback's dashed edge), `--x-proposed-future` and `--x-proposed-future-wash` (the premortem's far plane). Carried and still owed: rung inks 0 to 4, pencil and pencil wash, lit and lit wash, seam, the unseen hatch, disagree and its wash, quiet, micro type. `--font-mono` still needs an ADR.
- *Kit parts.* The technique sheet; the result label (chip, card, page); the offer card; the assumption mark; the explanations grid with sticky headers; the shared time rail (signposts, tripwires and the decision's life should all use it).
- *Not prototyped.* A technique run over several days by people in different places; a technique on the Team or Org floor; check the sources and the case against as full surfaces.

**Data the shell and apps don't have yet:**
- A **`working` result record** with its nine fields, cited by `Ref` from briefings, intents and decisions.
- **Facts with their value and word at a moment** (the minute run's "value at decision time"), so a result keeps what its techniques read.
- **Source authorship** ("written by the subject") and **independence** ("derived from"), for the margin rules and linchpin pairs.
- A **contract outcomes register with a reason** for each failure, from a controlled list: the reference class.
- **Assumptions on plans**, each with load, sign, hedge, owner, and a "lowered by, because".
- **Pause points per decision kind**, and a "skipped, because" state.
- **Sentence-level citations** in Artifacts, for offers in the margin and results attached to sentences.
- **Timing per run** (start, finish, people) and **outcomes** per assumption, reason and forecast.
- **Shared group state** for the premortem's write-alone round.
- The events service's **clock** and **silence** events.
