# Concepts found by this run

Each is bigger than one prototype and could steer a future run. They are also filed in `design-lab/concepts/`.

## What doesn't fit decides (`what-doesnt-fit-decides`)
An explanation is judged only by the facts against it, never by how many fit. Facts that fit everything are grey (they can't tell explanations apart); an untested explanation can't win; two left standing is a result, with the one fact that would split them named. Evidence: explanations V1, V2, V3 and V4, combined V1; Heuer (1999). Could steer: Data Atlas triage, the argument (Z), incident reviews.

## Below the bar, with a hedge (`hedged-below-the-bar`)
A decision that must be taken before it can be proven may go ahead below its kind's standard of proof only if every assumption below the bar has a written hedge and a sign that would trigger it, agreed at the table. The decision says so, and names its weakest assumption. Evidence: assumptions V5, kept V1, combined V1 and V3; Dewar (RAND 1993), Munger's inversion. Could steer: Mandate decisions, approvals, Upgrade Centre. Needs Harry's decision.

## Keep the score (`keep-the-score`)
Every tested judgement is scored when the future arrives (held, partly, didn't, can't tell), for floors and techniques, never people. A technique counts as paid only when it changed a line; one that rarely does leaves the shelf for that floor. Evidence: kept V3, grammar V5, choose V5, combined V4; Tetlock and Gardner (2015). Could steer: the annotated game (W6), forecasts in Explorer, the decision log.

## The model widens, never narrows (`the-model-widens-never-narrows`)
A model may add possibilities (an explanation, a reason, the case against, a fact to find) and never remove or rank them (mark, rank, drop, choose, set a word). What it adds arrives empty and untested. Evidence: grammar V6, explanations V6, assumptions V3 and V6, premortem V1, kept V6. Could steer: every model-gateway feature, Data Atlas triage, the argument (Z).

## Start from how it usually goes (`start-from-the-reference-class`)
Any forecast, term or deadline starts from a class of the organisation's own past cases, shown as a count, with every move from the count a separate stated step; the forecast is kept to be scored and becomes one more case. Evidence: combined V1 stage 1, premortem V5, choose V4; Kahneman and Lovallo (1993). Could steer: Mandate deadlines, Upgrade Centre, a contract outcomes register in Data Atlas.

## Pause points (`pause-points`)
Each kind of decision has a few fixed moments where a technique is offered (question set, plan on paper, before signing); between them nothing is offered, and a skipped one says why. Evidence: choose V3 and V2, combined V1; WHO surgical safety checklist (2009). Could steer: Mandate decision kinds, approvals, the commission (V). Needs Harry's decision.

Also advanced:
- **the technique fits the question** (to explore → explored): six kinds of question, one technique each, offered one at a time by rule; the key, the four situations ("no technique" is an answer), and the bench as a kit part (grammar V2 and V3, choose V2, combined V2).
- **the workings show**: the result label, nine fields kept beside the output (grammar V4, kept V1).
- **consequence preview**: the live draft changing as each technique changes a line, and "what you give up" on a cheaper technique (combined V1, choose V6).
- **the decision sets the bar**: extended by hedged below the bar (assumptions V5).
- **confidence is worked out**: assumption marks are worked out from the fact's word by the same rule; a challenge may only lower a mark (assumptions V1 and V6).
- **watches say what they can't see**: signposts and tripwires drawn even when AUGUR can't see them; the 12-day miss on a date passing (assumptions V4, premortem V6, combined V3).
- **revisit lines**: assumptions' signs and premortem watches are the revisit lines; a crossing offers the hedge written at the table (combined V3).
- **pencil and ink**: the model's explanation arrives as an empty column (explanations V6).
- **ask only what changes**: AUGUR reads the intent and asks one question (choose V2).
- **decided versus said**: assumptions found in the plan's own words (assumptions V3).
- **dark when normal**: uncrossed tripwires draw only their line (premortem V6).

Related concepts from the parallel argument run (Z): *the warrant shows* is the structure half of what this run does for method; a warrant that broke is an assumption that failed.
