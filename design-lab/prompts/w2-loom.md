# Wildcard W2: Loom. Structure and work as warp and weft.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-loom/prompt.md`.

---

Weaving is one of the oldest information technologies: a fixed set of
lengthwise threads (the warp) held under tension, and a crosswise thread
(the weft) carried back and forth to make cloth. Weavers plan cloth on a
**draft**, a grid that records threading, tie-up and treadling, and the
Jacquard loom ran patterns from punched cards that inspired early
computing. This wildcard asks whether AUGUR's structure (floors, teams,
apps: fixed, under tension) and its work (requests, documents, facts that
travel across them) read better as warp and weft. It may argue against any
existing concept if the research supports it. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-loom/`.

## Direction
- **Structure is warp, work is weft.** Show where work crosses structure,
  and where it floats over without binding (a "float" in weaving is a
  thread that skips, a weak spot).
- **The draft is the plan, the cloth is the result.** Show both, and where
  they differ.
- **Selvedge**: the finished edge that stops cloth unravelling. What is
  AUGUR's selvedge?
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Anni Albers, *On Weaving*** (1965) and her pictorial weavings. Take:
  structure as the image.
- **Weaving drafts** (threading, tie-up, treadling, drawdown). Take: a
  grid that predicts a pattern from rules.
- **The Jacquard loom and its punched cards**; Ada Lovelace's note that the
  Analytical Engine "weaves algebraic patterns". Take: pattern as program.
- **Textile conservation**: reading damage, repairs and wear in cloth.
  Take: the link to visible repair.
- **Kente, ikat and tartan setts**: patterns that carry identity and rank.
  Take: floors and departments recognisable by their weave.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Weave sheet** (grammar): warp, weft, float, selvedge, draft,
   drawdown, sett, and what each means in AUGUR.
2. **The org as cloth** (Explorer): floors and teams as warp, work as
   weft, floats as gaps where work crosses without anyone binding it.
3. **A Mandate intent as a draft and its drawdown**: plan against result.
4. **Floor identity as a sett**: each floor's pattern, readable at a
   glance in the floor mark and title.
5. **Handover as a crossing**: the point where one thread passes to
   another.
6. **Brand surface**: the product name proposal (Skein) worked as a weave,
   for the title and empty states.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must show the same org month as
cloth and as a plain list, and say honestly which reads better.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Does the metaphor survive people who have never seen a loom?
- Which weave ideas are structural and which are only decoration?
- Is a "float" (work crossing without binding) a real, measurable gap in
  AUGUR's data?
