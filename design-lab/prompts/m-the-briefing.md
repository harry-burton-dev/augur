# Prompt M: The briefing. From data to a decision.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-briefing/prompt.md`.

---

The point of keeping data is to use it: to brief a lead, decide something,
spot a change in time. Operators rarely need a dashboard; they need the
two sentences that matter, a picture they can trust, and the details one
step away when someone asks "how do you know?". This run asks how AUGUR
**presents and abstracts** data so non-technical people can exploit it:
from a single fact to a briefing a manager reads in a minute, at the right
level of detail for each reader. Picture statisticians, military staff and
science communicators have practised this for a century. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-briefing/`.

## Direction
- **Bottom line first.** Every view of data can say its point in a
  sentence before it shows a chart.
- **One fact, three heights:** the same knowledge as a headline, a
  picture and the records, moving up and down the ladder of abstraction.
- **Uncertainty in plain words** ("likely", "we have three sources, one
  disagrees"), never hidden in a footnote.
- **Using it is one step:** a finding can become a briefing in Artifacts,
  feed an intent in Mandate, or be watched for change.
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Otto and Marie Neurath's Isotype** and the role of the
  "transformer", who turned statistics into pictures for the public.
  Take: the transformer as a role (and maybe a tool), and counting with
  repeated symbols rather than scaled ones.
- **BLUF and staff briefing practice** (bottom line up front; the
  military appreciation). Take: the order a busy reader needs.
- **Hans Rosling and Our World in Data.** Take: one clear claim per chart,
  and the chart's title as the finding.
- **Bret Victor, "Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction"**, and
  **explorable explanations**. Take: moving between a summary and its
  cases without losing your place.
- **The IPCC's calibrated uncertainty language** ("likely" = 66-100%) and
  the UK intelligence "probability yardstick". Take: a fixed vocabulary
  for confidence that lay readers understand.
- **Tufte's sparklines and small multiples.** Take: pictures at word size,
  inside sentences.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Briefing sheet** (grammar): bottom line, picture, workings, height
   (headline, picture, records), confidence word; token recipes.
2. **The fact at three heights**: one entity's key fact as a sentence,
   a picture and its records, moving between them in place.
3. **The one-minute briefing**: a generated or hand-made briefing on a
   topic, readable by a manager in a minute, every claim traceable.
4. **Choosing the picture**: an operator turns a set of records into the
   right chart, guided by the question rather than a chart picker.
5. **Confidence for everyone**: the yardstick words applied to facts,
   disputes and gaps across a briefing.
6. **From finding to action**: publish to Artifacts, feed an intent,
   or watch for change, with the reach shown first.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must show the same briefing for
three readers (the operator, their team lead, the Org), each at its own
height, all from the same data.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Which charts an operator should be offered at all (and which never).
- Where a model may write the bottom line, and how its claims stay tied
  to records.
- What "watch for change" needs from Data Atlas and the events service.
