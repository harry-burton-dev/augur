# Prompt F: The score. Many people playing one piece of work.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-score/prompt.md`.

---

An orchestra of eighty plays one piece in time from paper. Every player has
a part with only what they need; the conductor has the full score; rehearsal
marks let anyone say "from letter C"; rests and cues tell you when you're
not needed and when to come in. Dancers are notated the same way. This run
asks what AUGUR looks like if work across a team or an organisation is
treated as a **score**: parts and the full score, cues, rests and rehearsal
marks, on the one time axis the wear and time run proposed. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-score/`.

## Direction
- **Your part, and the full score.** Each person sees their own part; a
  lead sees everyone's on one page, aligned in time.
- **Rests are information.** "Nothing for you until Thursday" is as useful
  as a task.
- **Cues, not pings.** You are told to come in by what happens before your
  entry (someone else's step finishing), shown in your part.
- **Rehearsal marks** give any moment an address people can say aloud
  (time-as-address).
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Orchestral scores and parts**: systems, staves, part extraction,
  cue notes, multi-bar rests, rehearsal letters, tacet. Take: part vs full
  score, cue notes, multi-bar rests.
- **Graphic scores, especially Cornelius Cardew's *Treatise*** (1963-67),
  and Morton Feldman's grid scores: notation as information design, where
  shape carries instruction. Take: how far a score can go before it stops
  being readable.
- **Labanotation and Benesh notation**: a vertical staff per body, time
  running up the page, symbols for direction, level and duration. Take: a
  staff per person or team.
- **The conductor's role**: beat, cue, dynamics, cut-off. Take: what a
  lead actually does on a shared timeline.
- **Marey's train graph** (already used in the wear and time run, upgrades
  V4): lines through time and place. Take: compare it with the score.
- **Theatre prompt books and cue sheets** ("standby", "go"). Take: the
  two-step call for anything irreversible.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Notation sheet** (grammar): staff, part, full score, rest, cue,
   rehearsal mark, dynamics (urgency), cut-off, standby and go.
2. **Mandate intent as a score**: the teams and people an intent needs,
   each a staff, with entries, rests and cues.
3. **Your part** (Home): what you play today, the rests ahead, the cue you
   are waiting for.
4. **Upgrade rollout as orchestration**: rings and services as parts,
   standby and go before each step.
5. **Team floor as a full score**: a week of a team's work on one page.
6. **Handover as a cue**: the moment one person's part ends and the next
   one's begins.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must show the same piece of work as
a full score and as two different people's parts, all three in step.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Which notation ideas survive for people who don't read music?
- What AUGUR would need to know about dependencies between steps for cues
  to work (the cinematic and wear and time runs also hit this gap).
- Where this agrees or clashes with one-now and the intent ledger.
