# Prompt S: The edition. Publishing a document people can rely on.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-edition/prompt.md`.

---

Batch 5 is about **work outputs**: what operators make with AUGUR and
hand to someone else (a published document, a decision, a case bundle, a
request fulfilled, a filled-in form). AUGUR's pillar apps hold the pieces
(Artifacts, Mandate, Data Atlas); these prompts ask how a finished piece of
work is made, checked, delivered, and trusted later, across apps.

Artifacts already has drafts, publishing to a floor, superseding and
withdrawing (docs/folio.md). This run asks what a **published document**
should be as an output: an edition with a date and a number, a known
audience, corrections that stay visible, a clear successor when it is
replaced, and readers who are told when something they rely on changes.
Newspapers, standards bodies and legislators have practised this for
centuries. Work in `design-lab/runs/<date>-the-edition/`.

## Direction
- **An edition is fixed:** once published it doesn't change silently;
  a correction is a new, visible entry (visible repair).
- **Its status is on the page:** draft, in review, published, superseded,
  withdrawn, each with who and when.
- **Readers who rely on it hear about changes,** and only they.
- **Reach is shown before publishing** (hear the reach).
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Newspaper editions, errata and corrections columns.** Take: the
  correction as a public, dated entry.
- **Standards bodies** (BSI, ISO: drafts for comment, published
  standards, amendments, withdrawn and superseded). Take: a lifecycle
  with names everyone knows.
- **Legislation.gov.uk** (original and revised versions, prospective
  changes). Take: reading a document as it stood on a date.
- **The printing house colophon and imprint.** Take: who made it, when,
  where, from what.
- **Retraction Watch and journal retractions.** Take: what a withdrawn
  output must still say.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Edition sheet** (grammar): draft, proof, edition, imprint,
   correction, amendment, supersede, withdraw.
2. **Going to press**: the publish moment with reach, review and imprint.
3. **The published page**: status, edition and corrections on the page.
4. **A correction**: issuing it, and who is told.
5. **Superseded and withdrawn**: what the old edition says now.
6. **What I rely on**: a reader's list of editions they cite, and changes.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must follow one policy document
through two editions and a correction, as seen by its author and a reader
who quoted it.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- What Artifacts records today and what an edition model would add.
- How "rely on" is recorded without tracking reading (mine versus ours).
- How this agrees with wear and time's seams and the label run (L).
