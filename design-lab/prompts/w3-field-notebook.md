# Wildcard W3: The field notebook. Data made by the people who see it.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-field-notebook/prompt.md`.

---

The rest of batch 3 treats operators as users of data someone else
keeps. This wildcard turns that round: operators are the people who
**see things first**, and the best data an organisation has may be what
they notice and write down. Naturalists have run this for over a
century. Joseph Grinnell's system (a field notebook, a journal, species
accounts and a catalogue) turned observations into records still used by
scientists today, and citizen science projects like eBird and iNaturalist
get millions of non-experts to record data good enough for research. This
run asks what AUGUR looks like if capturing an observation is as natural
as jotting a note, and the record improves as others confirm it. It may
argue against any existing concept, including Triage as it stands, if the
research supports it. Work in `design-lab/runs/<date>-field-notebook/`.

## Direction
- **Jot first, structure later.** An observation starts as a note in the
  person's words, and becomes a record with help.
- **Agreement raises quality:** others confirm, question or correct, as
  iNaturalist's "research grade" does, and the record shows it.
- **Effort is recorded too:** eBird's "complete checklist" flag says
  whether absence means "not there" or "not looked for".
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **The Grinnell system** (Museum of Vertebrate Zoology, Berkeley):
  field notebook, journal, species account, catalogue. Take: four layers
  from raw note to kept record.
- **eBird checklists**: location, time, effort, and "is this a complete
  list?". Take: recording how hard you looked, so absence means something.
- **iNaturalist's identification and research grade.** Take: community
  agreement as a visible quality level.
- **Ship's logs and police pocket notebooks**: contemporaneous notes with
  rules (no erasures, signed, timed). Take: notes that can be trusted
  later.
- **Commonplace books.** Take: a personal store of noticings that feeds
  shared knowledge.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Notebook sheet** (grammar): note, journal, account, catalogue,
   effort, agreement; what each means in AUGUR.
2. **Jotting an observation**: from anywhere in AUGUR, in a few seconds,
   with place and time captured for you.
3. **From note to record**: the help that turns a note into a proposal
   for Data Atlas, and what stays a note.
4. **Agreement**: colleagues confirm or question an observation, and its
   grade changes on the record.
5. **Effort and absence**: "we looked and found nothing" as data.
6. **A person's notebook**: everything someone has noticed, and what
   became of it.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must follow a single observation
from a jotted note to a confirmed fact used in a briefing.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Does this replace Triage for people's observations, or feed it?
- What stops a notebook becoming noise?
- What is recorded about effort and agreement that Data Atlas doesn't
  keep today?
