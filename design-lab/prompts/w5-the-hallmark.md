# Wildcard W5: The hallmark. Marks of quality and origin on finished work.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-hallmark/prompt.md`.

---

Batch 5 is about work outputs. This wildcard asks how someone receiving an
output, perhaps years later, can tell **who made it, to what standard,
who checked it and when**, at a glance. Silversmiths have done this since
1300: a British hallmark carries the maker's mark, a standard mark, the
assay office's mark and a date letter, struck by an independent office.
Masons left marks on stones; printers left colophons; engineers stamp
drawings. This run asks what AUGUR outputs would look like with
hallmarks. It may argue against any existing concept if the research
supports it. Work in `design-lab/runs/<date>-the-hallmark/`.

## Direction
- **A few marks, always in the same order,** read at a glance.
- **The checker is not the maker:** the mark that matters is struck by
  someone else.
- **A mark can't be faked or silently moved** (signed, recorded).
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **British hallmarking** (Hallmarking Act 1973; the four marks; assay
  offices). Take: maker, standard, checker, date.
- **Masons' marks and printers' colophons.** Take: the maker's own mark.
- **Professional engineers' stamps and drawing title blocks** (drawn,
  checked, approved, revision). Take: the title block as a hallmark.
- **Kitemarks and CE marking.** Take: standard marks people trust.
- **Digital signatures and content credentials (C2PA).** Take: marks
  that can be verified by machine.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Hallmark sheet** (grammar): maker, standard, checker, date, office,
   revision.
2. **The mark on a document**: a published output with its hallmark.
3. **Striking the mark**: the check by someone other than the maker.
4. **Reading an old output**: what the marks say years later.
5. **The title block**: drawn, checked, approved, revision, on any output.
6. **A broken mark**: an output changed after it was marked.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must show the same output from three
floors' eyes, each reading its marks.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Which outputs deserve a hallmark, and which would make it noise.
- Who may act as an assay office in an organisation.
- What signing (C2PA or the release feed's signing) AUGUR could reuse.
