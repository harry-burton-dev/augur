# Prompt AB: The situation. From data to information to context.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-situation/prompt.md`.

---

Batch 6 is about **thinking made visible**. This prompt is about the
climb from data to information to context.

Data is a reading: "27 missed collections in October". Information is
data given meaning: "missed collections doubled since July". Context is
information placed against **who is asking, what they are trying to do
and what else is going on**: "for the contract renewal due 1 December,
this is the strongest evidence against renewing, and the supplier's own
count disagrees". AUGUR holds the first two well (Data Atlas, Explorer,
Artifacts). The third is assembled by hand, in people's heads, every
time, and lost when they move on. This run asks how AUGUR could make
**context a thing you can see, check, hand over and keep**: the working
set a person (or an agent) needs for a task, assembled from what AUGUR
holds, with each piece saying why it is there. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-situation/`.

## Direction
- **Name the rung.** Every item shows whether it is data, information or
  context, and what lifted it a rung (a comparison, a threshold, a
  question, an intent).
- **Context is a working set**: a reviewable object made for a task, with
  its own owner, date and reason for each piece, not a hidden retrieval
  step.
- **Relative to someone.** The same information is different context for
  a Team lead, a Department head and the Org; context names its reader.
- **Frames are explicit.** When two people read the same data
  differently, show both frames rather than averaging them.
- **Context goes stale.** It says when it was assembled and what has
  changed since.
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Ackoff, "From data to wisdom" (1989)** and the DIKW hierarchy, with
  its critics (Frické; Weinberger). Take: the rungs as vocabulary, and
  the warning that the climb isn't automatic.
- **Gregory Bateson, "a difference which makes a difference"** (*Steps to
  an Ecology of Mind*, 1972). Take: information is a difference relative
  to a reader.
- **Mica Endsley's situation awareness (1995)**: perception,
  comprehension, projection. Take: three levels a screen can support
  separately, and the third (what happens next) as the one most often
  missing.
- **Klein, Moon and Hoffman's data/frame theory of sensemaking (2006)**
  and Karl Weick's *Sensemaking in Organizations* (1995). Take: people
  fit data to a frame, and question the frame when the data won't fit.
- **Freeman Tilden, *Interpreting Our Heritage* (1957)** and museum
  interpretation. Take: information alone isn't interpretation; relate
  it to the visitor.
- **The journalist's nut graf and "why it matters"**, and the military
  **common operational picture**. Take: the paragraph that says why this
  matters to this reader now, and one shared picture each role filters.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **The ladder** (grammar): data, information, context, with what lifts
   each rung and how each looks.
2. **One reading, three rungs**: a single figure raised from data to
   information to context, step by step.
3. **The working set**: the context assembled for one task (a renewal
   decision), as an object a person can review, prune and hand over.
4. **Same information, three readers**: the context for a Team lead, a
   Department head and the Org from the same facts.
5. **Two frames**: two people reading the same data differently, shown
   side by side with what would settle it.
6. **Stale context**: a working set opened three weeks later, with what
   has changed and what no longer holds.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must follow a working set from
assembly, through a handover to a new owner who wasn't there, to a
decision, with one piece of context going stale on the way.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Is a working set a kind of Artifacts document, a Data Atlas query, a
  Mandate attachment, or its own object? Who owns it?
- What a working set made for an agent must contain so a person can
  review what the agent was given (and how this agrees with the
  apprentice, Q).
- What AUGUR must record about each piece (source, rung, reason,
  reader, date) and which existing services hold it.
