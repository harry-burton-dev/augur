# Prompt Z: The argument. Reasoning a reader can check.

Read `design-lab/brief.md` first; it holds the rules, kit, critique format
and run shape. When you start, move this file to
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-argument/prompt.md`.

---

Batch 6 is about **thinking made visible**. This prompt is about logic:
the structure of reasoning inside a piece of knowledge work.

Most outputs an operator makes (a recommendation, a briefing, a case for
funding, an answer to a question from the Org) are arguments: a claim,
the evidence for it, and a reason the evidence supports the claim. Today
that structure lives only in the author's head and the reader has to
reverse-engineer it from prose. Lawyers, philosophers and consultants
have notations that make it explicit. This run asks how AUGUR could help
a non-specialist **build an argument that shows its structure**, and help
a reader **check it** without retracing the author's work. Work in
`design-lab/runs/<date>-the-argument/`.

## Direction
- **Claim, grounds, warrant.** Every claim shows what supports it and
  why that support counts (the warrant), not only a citation.
- **The weakest link is visible.** A claim is no stronger than its
  weakest supporting step, and the page says which step that is.
- **Objections are part of the argument**, shown and answered or left
  open, never cut to tidy the page.
- **The prose is a view of the structure**, not a separate thing: edit
  the argument and the text follows, or edit the text and the structure
  updates.
- **A model may draft, never decide.** Following pencil and ink, a model
  can suggest a missing warrant or an objection, in pencil; a person
  inks it.
- Explain, never hide; many deployments; calm at rest (from the brief).

## References and what to take from each
- **Stephen Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (1958)**: claim, grounds,
  warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal. Take: the six parts as the
  grammar, and the qualifier ("probably", "unless") as a first-class
  part.
- **Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* (1987)**: the answer first,
  grouped reasons beneath, situation-complication-question-answer, MECE
  groups. Take: the top-down shape of a written recommendation.
- **John Henry Wigmore's chart method (1913)** for legal evidence. Take:
  mapping many small pieces of evidence onto the facts they support or
  undermine.
- **Argument mapping** (Rationale, Kialo, van Gelder's work on critical
  thinking). Take: box-and-arrow maps non-specialists can read, with
  "because", "but" and "however" as the only connectors.
- **IBIS: issue-based information systems** (Kunz and Rittel, 1970;
  Compendium, dialogue mapping). Take: questions, positions and
  arguments as the record of how a group reasoned.
- **Legal IRAC and the judgment** (issue, rule, application, conclusion).
  Take: the rule applied as a visible step.

## Surfaces (6 variants each)
1. **Argument sheet** (grammar): claim, grounds, warrant, backing,
   qualifier, rebuttal, the weakest link, pencil and ink.
2. **Building a case**: an operator turns notes and Data Atlas facts into
   a recommendation for a Mandate intent.
3. **Reading a case**: a manager checks the argument in a minute and
   finds the weak step.
4. **Objections**: a colleague raises one; it attaches to the step it
   attacks and stays until answered.
5. **The pyramid and the prose**: one argument as structure and as a
   finished Artifacts document, kept in step.
6. **An argument over time**: new evidence arrives and the claim's
   strength changes, with the step that changed marked.

Round 2: combine into 3-4 screens. One must take a recommendation from
first notes to a signed decision, with one objection raised and answered
and one left open.

## Questions the shortlist must answer
- Is the argument a kind of Artifacts document, a structure any pillar
  app can carry, or a kit part?
- Which established open formats (for example the Argument Interchange
  Format) could hold it, and what an ADR would need to say.
- How this sits with the minute (T), the briefing (M) and confidence is
  worked out.
